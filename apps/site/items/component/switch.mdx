## Overview

A Switch turns a single setting on or off, and the change takes effect at once.
It renders a styled track with a hidden native checkbox beside it, so it submits
with a form while reading as a physical toggle. Space toggles the focused
control, and its on and off states are announced to assistive technology.

The difference from a Checkbox is timing, not shape. A Switch is a setting that
is already in force; a Checkbox is a choice collected before a save.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Switch when flipping the control applies the change immediately and the
reader expects no further step: enabling notifications, publishing a preview, or
turning a feature on.

### When not to use

Do not use a Switch inside a form that still needs a Save action. If the change
is pending a submit, a Checkbox is the honest control.

Do not use one to choose between two named options, such as Light and Dark. That
is a choice between values, and a RadioGroup or a Select names both.

Do not use a Switch for an action that cannot be undone with the same control. A
one-way action is a Button.

### Best practices

- Label the setting, not the state. The label describes what the switch
  controls; the on and off positions are conveyed by the control itself.
- Pair each Switch with a `FieldLabel` and `htmlFor`, and put the consequence in
  an adjacent helper line rather than in the label.
- For a change that affects a shared resource, confirm or offer an undo. An
  immediate setting with wide effect is easy to flip by accident.
- Keep the on state visually distinct beyond position alone, so the state is
  legible to a reader who cannot rely on the thumb's movement.
- Use `disabled` with a reason in reach, and prefer an explanatory line over a
  silent greyed row.

### Content guidelines

- Write the label as the thing being controlled, in sentence case: "Release
  notifications", "Preview builds".
- Keep helper text to one sentence that states the effect, including who is
  affected when it is shared.
- Avoid "Enable" or "Turn on" in the label; the control already says that.
