## Overview

A MoneyField holds a currency amount as a number and shows it as the reader's market
writes it. It is a Client Component.

The number the caller holds and the string the reader sees are different kinds of
thing, and the round trip between them is the whole Component. The caller passes a
`number` and gets a `number` back; the field draws the platform's own rendering of
that number in the caller's locale and the caller's currency; and every character the
reader adds is parsed back into one. A field that handed the caller a string would
push the parsing into every consumer, and a field that printed a hardcoded
`$1,234.56` would be making three claims about every product that installed it.

## The currency symbol is an input, not an output

It sits in the field's own frame, drawn by `InputGroupAddon`, read out of
`Intl.NumberFormat` rather than written anywhere in the Component, and hidden from the
accessibility tree. A screen reader reads the digits, and the currency belongs in the
field's accessible name, which is your word.

It is also not part of the value. The number a caller holds is `1234.56` whatever the
reader sees on screen, and a symbol that leaked into the parse would make the same
amount parse two ways depending on whether the reader typed it.

Nothing about a currency is a literal here. The symbol, the grouping and the decimal
separators, and the number of fraction digits all come out of the platform for the
locale and the code you passed. `de-DE` with `EUR` and `en-US` with `USD` differ in all
four.

## A partially typed amount is a legitimate state

`12.`, `-` and `.5` are all things a person is in the middle of typing, and `12,` and
`,5` are the same three in a locale that separates with a comma. So the parser is not
asked whether the text is a valid amount, only whether it currently means one. It
reports `12` for `12.`, nothing for a lone sign and for a lone separator, and `0.5`
for `.5`.

The text is never rewritten while the field has focus, because a field that re-formats
under the reader moves the caret and eats the character being typed. That is what
makes these states survivable at all.

The two values disagree for the whole of a mid-edit, and the Component resolves it in
one direction: your number is always the parse of the text, and the text is never
rewritten until the reader leaves. If you also render the amount somewhere else on the
page you will see the parsed value and not the keystrokes, and that is deliberate.

## Usage

```tsx
import { MoneyField } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/money-field'

const [amount, setAmount] = useState<number | null>(1234.56)

<MoneyField
  id="amount"
  aria-label="Amount in US dollars"
  locale="en-US"
  currency="USD"
  value={amount}
  onValueChange={setAmount}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it wherever a reader enters money: an invoice total, a budget, a price override, a
refund amount. It is the Component for a figure whose unit is a currency.

Pass `locale` on a page that serves more than one language. It is required, because a
locale is a claim about your market and this Component will not guess one.

Pass the currency as an ISO 4217 code rather than a symbol. The symbol is read back out
of the platform, so no two products can disagree about it, and a code is the one
currency name your data already holds.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a figure that is not money. A token count, a quota and a duration are
numbers with units, and a currency symbol on any of them is a claim about what they are.

Do not use it to collect an amount the reader does not type: the plan they chose or the
invoice they paid. That is `Price`, which prints rather than parses.

Do not rely on the form submission. There is deliberately no `name`: what a form would
submit from here is the reader's text with this locale's punctuation in it. Write a
hidden input holding `value` if you need a numeric submit.

Do not expect a pasted amount from another market to be read as that market wrote it.
`1,234.56` pasted into a `de-DE` field is `1.23456` here, because `,` is this locale's
decimal separator. No Component can do better from a string with no locale in it, so
pass the number in and let the field format it.

### Best practices

- Treat `null` as empty rather than as zero. It is reported on every keystroke, and it
  is reported for a text that currently means no amount, which includes a partially
  typed one.
- Expect the text to be rewritten on blur, and expect text that is not an amount to be
  refused. A reader who typed something this locale cannot write loses what they typed
  rather than being told it was wrong; that refusal is what keeps an amount field from
  ever holding something that is not an amount. Use a `FieldError` beside a `value` of
  `null` when you would rather explain it.
- Expect a field nobody touched to report nothing on blur. Tabbing through an amount
  without editing it is not a field resolving, and the value you hold stays the value
  you held.
- Treat `required` as announced rather than enforced. It is `aria-required`, because
  the browser's own refusal to submit comes with a message in English. Enforce it in
  your own submit handler, where it can be said in your language.
- Expect grouping characters to be dropped rather than accepted. They are cosmetic, and
  dropping one leaves the same digits, which is exactly the amount the reader meant.
- Use `describedBy` for the sentence about the field, such as "We will take this from
  the workspace balance", and keep the visible label on a `Label`.
- Override `inputMode` only if your market really types amounts some other way. The
  default is a `decimal` keyboard, because a `numeric` keyboard on a phone has no
  decimal point at all.
- Use `className` for width and placement only.

### Content guidelines

- Write `aria-label` with the currency in it, because the symbol is hidden from the
  accessibility tree: "Amount in US dollars", not "Amount". The visible `Label` and the
  announced name should say the same thing, since a field that looks localised and
  announces English is the state this Component exists to end.
