## Overview

A Calendar is a month grid for picking one date. It is built here rather than
composed, because Base UI at 1.8.0 ships a combobox, a number field, an OTP field
and a field, and no calendar. That is worth saying rather than implying, because
"the docs show one" is not evidence and a wrapper around a module that is not in
the installed package is the failure this Component could have shipped.

Three decisions carry the weight.

**The grid is always six weeks tall.** A month that needs five rows and a month
that needs six would otherwise resize as the reader pages through, moving the dates
under the pointer and under the highlight. The cells that pad the month out are
hidden from assistive technology rather than filled with the neighbouring month's
dates, because a date from another month drawn in this grid is a date the reader
did not come for.

**Every date, weekday name and month name is the caller's.** The caption, the seven
column headings and the string for each day are all reader-facing text in the
reader's language. What the Component carries is the geometry: which column a date
falls in and which day it sits on.

**A disabled date is drawn, reachable and refused.** It stays in the grid and stays
in the arrow path, announced as unavailable and refusing to be chosen. A date that
is simply missing is indistinguishable from a grid that failed to draw that week.

The month is a controlled prop, because the first month a calendar may open on is a
rule about the reader's situation rather than about the calendar: a booking form
opens on next month, an archive opens on the month the record is in.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Calendar } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/calendar'

const monthLabel = (month: Date) =>
  new Intl.DateTimeFormat(undefined, { month: 'long', year: 'numeric' }).format(month)

<Calendar
  label="Delivery date"
  monthLabel={monthLabel(month)}
  weekdayLabels={['Mon', 'Tue', 'Wed', 'Thu', 'Fri', 'Sat', 'Sun']}
  firstWeekday={1}
  previousLabel="Previous month"
  nextLabel="Next month"
  month={month}
  onMonthChange={setMonth}
  value={date}
  onValueChange={setDate}
  isDateDisabled={(candidate) => candidate.getDay() === 0}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Calendar when one date is the answer and the reader benefits from seeing the
shape of the month around it: a booking, a delivery date, a due date, a birthday.
Use a `DatePicker` when the date is the answer to a form field; this is the grid
that field opens.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a range. Two months, a start and an end, and a disabled range in
between are three more questions, and a single-month grid that answers them by
clicking twice gives the reader no way back.

Do not use it for a date of birth, unless the reader is under a hundred and ten.
A calendar that navigates to 1955 by paging backwards is a worse control than a
text field with a masked input and a clear format.

Do not use it when the reader only needs "today" or "tomorrow". A button labelled
with the date is faster than a grid for a choice with two answers.

### Best practices

- Format the caption, the weekday headings and the day labels in the reader's
  locale with `Intl`, and pass them in. That is the Component's whole reason for
  not shipping a table of month names.
- Pass `firstWeekday` to match your `weekdayLabels` order. The two are one fact and
  the Component cannot infer either half of it.
- Pass `dayLabel` when a day cell's name should be a full date. Without it the cell
  is named by its number and its column heading, which is what grid navigation
  already conveys.
- Reach for `isDateDisabled` for the rules: closed days, blackout dates, dates
  already taken. Explain them in text beside the grid; a greyed cell on its own
  says only that it is unavailable.
- Set `name` to have the chosen date submitted as `YYYY-MM-DD`. That is one format
  the server can parse without reading the reader's display convention.
- A `DatePicker` seeds the grid's month from the chosen date each time it opens.
  If you build your own surface, do the same, or a reader re-picking a date in
  another month lands in the month the field was created in.

### Content guidelines

- Abbreviate the weekday headings to two or three characters, in the reader's
  convention. A full name in a seven-column header forces a narrower grid.
- Name the previous and next controls for what they do, not for where they point:
  "Previous month" rather than "Go left".
- Write the grid's `label` as the field it answers, not as the control: "Delivery
  date", not "Calendar".
