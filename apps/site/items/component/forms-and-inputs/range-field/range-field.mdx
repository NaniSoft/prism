## Overview

A Range field edits an interval: two thumbs on one track, the lower and the upper
bound, and a sentence that says how wide the interval is.

**The value is a tuple, so the single-value plumbing does not apply.** `Slider` takes
`value?: number` and `onValueChange?: (value: number) => void`: one number in, one
number out. `NumberField` is the same shape for a scalar the reader types. An interval
is two numbers whose relationship to each other is the answer, and no arrangement of
two `Slider`s produces it, because each would own a value, an accessible name and a
tab stop, and two independently focusable controls that know nothing about each other
are a pair that can cross. Writing that today means writing the plumbing twice and
writing the guard that stops the crossing yourself, and the guard's absence is
invisible until a form submits a negative span.

**Base UI's Slider is a range primitive already, and this Component uses it rather
than reimplementing one.** The check was made before the code: the root is declared
over a single number or an array of them, each thumb takes an index into that array,
and the root carries a minimum distance between values for the crossing guard, which
is what `minGap` forwards to. Composing two Prism `Slider`s was rejected on its
merits: it would carry the same internal dependency twice for one control and leave
the collision behaviour to whichever of the two answered last. So the two focusable
handles, the Home and End keys and the form integration are **inherited, not written
here**, and the reason to own the surface at all is the announcement.

**The announcement is the contribution.** Two range inputs side by side announce two
numbers. What a reader wants from a band is a sentence with a direction: which end is
the floor, which is the ceiling, and how wide it is. Each thumb is named by your
`boundLabel`, which is handed which end it is, and the span is announced once, from a
visually hidden sentence your `spanLabel` writes.

## Usage

```tsx
import { RangeField, type RangeValue } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/range-field'

const [retention, setRetention] = useState<RangeValue>([30, 90])

<RangeField
  label="Retention window, days"
  min={0}
  max={365}
  minGap={7}
  value={retention}
  onValueChange={setRetention}
  boundLabel={(bound, formatted) =>
    bound === 'start' ? `Earliest day, ${formatted}` : `Latest day, ${formatted}`
  }
  spanLabel={(span, formatted) => `Window is ${formatted} days wide.`}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it wherever the answer is a band rather than a figure: a retention window, a price
range a buyer will pay, a severity range on an alert, a reporting period. The two
bounds are chosen together and their relationship is part of the answer.

Use `minGap` whenever there is a real minimum width. It is the guard against a
negative span, and a negative span is a value a backend rejects and a reader cannot
explain.

### When not to use

Do not use it for one number. That is `Slider` for a value the reader drags and
`NumberField` for one they type.

Do not use it where the exact figures matter more than their relationship. Two typed
fields with a validation rule between them are better when a reader needs to see both
numbers at once and may have to type either.

Do not compose two `Slider`s to make one. You would end up with two tab stops, two
accessible names, no knowledge between them and no guard.

### Best practices

- Pass a tuple, `readonly [number, number]`, lowest first. It is a tuple rather than an
  array because the two numbers are not interchangeable and an array would let a
  caller pass them the wrong way round.
- `onValueChange` receives the whole pair, not the bound that moved, because the span
  is a fact about the two of them together and this Component has already done the
  subtraction.
- Use one formatter. `format` changes the presentation of the drawn bounds, the two
  thumb names and the hidden span sentence at once, so the number on screen and the
  number announced cannot disagree.
- Prism prints bare figures and writes no word beside them, because the words next to
  a bound are yours. A unit or a currency goes through `format`, which is the same
  route `Price` takes rather than a second prop for a suffix.
- `boundLabel` and `spanLabel` are both required. An unsaid span is not a neutral
  omission: it is the one figure in the control a reader cannot derive, and deriving
  it means holding two announced values and subtracting them while driving.
- Each thumb renders its own range input, so a form with a `name` on this field
  submits both bounds under it.

### Content guidelines

- Name each bound by its direction: "Earliest day", "Up to", "At least". The pair has
  to read as a sentence when the two names are read one after the other.
- Write `spanLabel` as a complete statement with the unit in it, because it is read on
  its own and out of the context of the field label.
- Put the unit in `label` when the bounds are plain numbers, the way the demo does, so
  the caption carries what the figures do not.
