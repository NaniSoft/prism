## Overview

A Sheet is a panel anchored to an edge of the viewport. It ships as `Sheet`,
`SheetTrigger`, `SheetContent`, `SheetHeader`, `SheetFooter`, `SheetTitle`,
`SheetDescription` and `SheetClose`.

It is the Dialog with an edge, and it inherits the Dialog's behaviour rather
than reimplementing it. Focus trapping, Escape, the portal, the scroll lock, the
backdrop and the return of focus to the trigger are six behaviours that are
already correct in the Dialog, and a panel that rolled its own overlay would be a
second answer to all six questions, with the second answer being the one that
ships the bug.

What the sheet adds is a decision a consumer would otherwise get wrong: a panel
has a side, and the side is named here rather than defaulted. `center` is not on
`SheetContent`'s type, because a centred panel is a Dialog wearing a different
name, and a drawer that arrives as a centred card is a product that believes it
shipped something it did not.

A sheet dismisses on an outside press, and that is the difference from an
AlertDialog. A sheet holds navigation, filters and detail views: a reader who
clicks away has said "not now", and the content behind is still there when they
come back.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  Sheet,
  SheetContent,
  SheetDescription,
  SheetHeader,
  SheetTitle,
  SheetTrigger,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/sheet'

<Sheet>
  <SheetTrigger>Open filters</SheetTrigger>
  <SheetContent side="right">
    <SheetHeader>
      <SheetTitle>Filters</SheetTitle>
      <SheetDescription>Narrow the table to the rows you need.</SheetDescription>
    </SheetHeader>
  </SheetContent>
</Sheet>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Sheet for a panel that belongs to the page behind it and should not hide
it: navigation on a narrow screen, a filter set, a detail view beside a list, an
account panel. The page stays visible and usable, and the panel sits over one
edge of it.

### When not to use

Do not use a Sheet for a task the reader has to finish before going back. A
rename, a short form and a confirmation belong in a Dialog, which puts them in
the middle where they cannot be mistaken for part of the page.

Do not use a Sheet for a destructive decision. It dismisses on an outside press,
and a stray click would answer a question the reader never read. Use an
AlertDialog, which refuses dismissal.

Do not use a Sheet for a full page. On a narrow screen a sheet at three quarters
of the width is a page with a backdrop, and a real page is better.

### Best practices

- Always render a `SheetTitle`. It names the panel for every reader.
- Name the side deliberately. `right` for a detail view beside a list, `bottom`
  for filters and actions on a phone, `left` for navigation.
- Keep the panel's width a fraction of the viewport rather than a fixed pixel
  count, so the page behind stays visible at every size.
- Put the primary action at the bottom of a bottom sheet and at the top of a side
  sheet, so it lands under the same thumb either way.
- Do not nest a Dialog inside a Sheet unless the dialog is a confirmation about
  the sheet's own content. A reader who cannot tell which layer is on top will
  dismiss the wrong one.
- The close control is rendered by default. Turn it off only when the panel has
  its own explicit way out.

### Content guidelines

- Make the title the thing the panel is for: "Filters", "Notifications",
  "Account".
- One sentence of description, saying what the panel changes about the page
  behind it.
- Put the panel's own controls first and the page's content second. A reader who
  opened a sheet is here to change something, not to read.
