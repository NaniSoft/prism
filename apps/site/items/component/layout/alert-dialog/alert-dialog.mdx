## Overview

An AlertDialog is a dialog that interrupts, and will not let the reader out of it
by accident. It ships as `AlertDialog`, `AlertDialogTrigger`,
`AlertDialogContent`, `AlertDialogHeader`, `AlertDialogFooter`,
`AlertDialogTitle`, `AlertDialogDescription`, `AlertDialogAction` and
`AlertDialogCancel`.

The difference from a Dialog is the whole Component. A Dialog closes on an
outside press, because a reader who clicks away from a task has said "not now"
and the task is still there when they come back. An alert dialog exists for a
decision that a mis-click would answer, so an outside press is refused outright:
Base UI hard-wires dismissal off and the root's prop type omits both `modal` and
`disablePointerDismissal`, so there is no value a caller can pass that turns a
must-answer decision into something a stray click waves through.

Escape still closes it, and returns focus to the trigger. A keyboard reader must
always have a way out, and Escape is a deliberate answer where a stray click is
not.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  AlertDialog,
  AlertDialogAction,
  AlertDialogCancel,
  AlertDialogContent,
  AlertDialogDescription,
  AlertDialogFooter,
  AlertDialogHeader,
  AlertDialogTitle,
  AlertDialogTrigger,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/alert-dialog'

<AlertDialog>
  <AlertDialogTrigger>Delete project</AlertDialogTrigger>
  <AlertDialogContent>
    <AlertDialogHeader>
      <AlertDialogTitle>Delete this project?</AlertDialogTitle>
      <AlertDialogDescription>
        Twelve runs and every log are removed. This cannot be undone.
      </AlertDialogDescription>
    </AlertDialogHeader>
    <AlertDialogFooter>
      <AlertDialogCancel>Keep it</AlertDialogCancel>
      <AlertDialogAction>Delete it</AlertDialogAction>
    </AlertDialogFooter>
  </AlertDialogContent>
</AlertDialog>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use an AlertDialog when the decision cannot safely be deferred, and where a
stray click would answer it wrongly: deleting a workspace, discarding a draft,
ending a session, closing a subscription. The bar is "would this reader be angry
if they dismissed this by accident", not "is this important".

### When not to use

Do not use an AlertDialog for a short task. A rename, a compact form and a
filter all belong in a Dialog, which dismisses on an outside press because "not
now" is a safe answer to a task.

Do not use it to deliver a message nobody has to answer. An alert with no
decision in it is an alert, and alerts interrupt; a message about something that
already happened belongs in a Toast or in the page.

Do not use it for a decision the reader can reverse. If they can press Back and
get the project back, a Dialog is the surface, and a surface that refuses
dismissal on a reversible action trains readers to press Escape reflexively,
which is exactly the reflex that makes the next one dangerous.

### Best practices

- Always render an `AlertDialogTitle`. It is the dialog's accessible name and it
  is what the surface is announced as.
- Render both buttons, always. The two are the only ways out besides Escape, and
  a dialog with one button is a notification.
- Put the action last in the footer, so it is where a keyboard reader lands
  second and the cancel is the one under their thumb when they change their mind.
- The action is styled `destructive` by default. Pass `variant="default"` for a
  decision that is not destructive, and reconsider whether the decision needed
  to interrupt at all.
- Name the action for what it does: "Delete it", not "Confirm". "Confirm"
  confirms whatever the reader last clicked, which is frequently not this.
- Open it from a menu item or a row action, not from a page-level button. A
  destructive action offered once per page gets clicked without being read.

### Content guidelines

- The title is the question, in the reader's words: "Delete this project?".
- The description is the consequence, in one sentence, before the buttons: what
  is removed, and whether it can be put back. A description that restates the
  title gives the reader a decision with nothing to decide on.
- Keep the words short. This is a surface a reader meets in the middle of
  something else, and a paragraph is a paragraph they will not finish.
