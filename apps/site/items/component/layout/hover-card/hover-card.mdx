## Overview

A HoverCard is a preview of a destination, shown when the reader rests on a
link. It ships as `HoverCard`, `HoverCardTrigger` and `HoverCardContent`.

The delay is the Component's decision, and both halves of it are props. A card
with no open delay opens as the pointer crosses a line of text, so a page of
links becomes a page of flashes and a reader learns that resting is punished. A
card with no close delay is worse and quieter: the reader rests, waits, sees the
card start to appear, moves toward it, and it vanishes in the gap between the
trigger and the card, so the one reader who did everything right gets nothing.
`delay` and `closeDelay` are props because how long a reader's pointer rests is a
fact about the reader and not about the design system.

The trigger is a real anchor, so everything in the card is reachable at the
destination without the card ever appearing. That is the honest way to express
"the reader wants this" without punishing a reader on a slow device: the card is
an accelerant for a reader with a pointer, and removing it costs that reader
nothing.

The card opens on focus as well as on hover, so a keyboard reader is not a second
class. It is not a dialog: it takes no focus, traps nothing, and is not announced,
because a screen reader is already moving through the link text.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  HoverCard,
  HoverCardContent,
  HoverCardTrigger,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/hover-card'

<p>
  The principles of good{' '}
  <HoverCard>
    <HoverCardTrigger href="/foundation/typography">
      typography
    </HoverCardTrigger>
    <HoverCardContent>
      <p>A system of arranging type so written language stays clear.</p>
    </HoverCardContent>
  </HoverCard>{' '}
  are older than the screen.
</p>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a HoverCard for a short preview of a destination the reader is about to
visit: what a person looks like before you follow, what a page contains before
you open it, what a repository is before you clone it. A few lines, readable at a
glance, gone the moment the pointer moves on.

### When not to use

Do not use it for anything a reader must read. A touch reader has no hover at
all, and a fact that lives only in a card does not exist for them.

Do not use it for a form, a menu or anything the reader operates. The card takes
no focus and traps nothing, so a control inside it is unreachable.

Do not use it where a Tooltip is the right answer. A tooltip names or explains
the control it is attached to in a few words. A hover card previews somewhere
else, and it is a link.

Do not use it as a substitute for page load. A card that fetches on hover and
arrives late is worse than a page that was already there.

### Best practices

- Put nothing in the card that is not also at the destination. That is what makes
  the card safe to add and the delay safe to be long.
- Keep it to a few lines. A preview the reader has to read is a page.
- Leave the defaults unless the content argues for a different delay. The default
  is a rest, and a rest is what a reader means by staying.
- If a link is important enough that its readers need the preview, the preview
  belongs in the page, not in a card.
- Set `delay` to zero only where the trigger is already obviously a preview
  surface, such as an avatar grid.

### Content guidelines

- Lead with the destination's own name, so the preview and the page agree.
- One or two lines. No headings, no lists longer than three items.
- No calls to action. The reader is either going to follow the link or they are
  not, and a card that argues for it is a card they have to dismiss.
