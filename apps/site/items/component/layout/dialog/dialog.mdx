## Overview

A Dialog is a modal surface for a decision or a short task. It ships as
`Dialog`, `DialogTrigger`, `DialogContent`, `DialogHeader`, `DialogFooter`,
`DialogTitle`, `DialogDescription` and `DialogClose`. It also covers the old
drawer pattern through the `side` prop on `DialogContent`: `center` is the modal
card, and `top`, `bottom`, `left` and `right` anchor the panel to an edge as a
sheet.

While it is open the dialog traps focus, locks page scroll, closes on Escape and
on an outside press, and returns focus to its trigger when it closes. Always
render a `DialogTitle`: it names the dialog and is announced on open.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  Dialog,
  DialogClose,
  DialogContent,
  DialogDescription,
  DialogFooter,
  DialogHeader,
  DialogTitle,
  DialogTrigger,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/dialog'

<Dialog>
  <DialogTrigger>Rename workspace</DialogTrigger>
  <DialogContent>
    <DialogHeader>
      <DialogTitle>Rename workspace</DialogTitle>
      <DialogDescription>The new name appears in every menu.</DialogDescription>
    </DialogHeader>
    <DialogFooter>
      <DialogClose>Cancel</DialogClose>
    </DialogFooter>
  </DialogContent>
</Dialog>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Dialog when the reader must stop and make a decision before continuing, or
when a short task needs focus without losing the page behind it: a confirmation,
a rename, or a compact form.

### When not to use

Do not use a Dialog for content that is not a task. Reading material or settings
belong on a page or in a Popover.

Do not stack dialogs, and do not use a centered Dialog for a long list or a
full-screen form. The side variant covers a sheet.

### Best practices

- Always include a `DialogTitle`. It is the dialog's accessible name, and
  without it a screen reader announces an unnamed dialog.
- Put the primary action last in `DialogFooter`, and keep one primary action per
  dialog.
- For a destructive action, state the consequence in the `DialogDescription`,
  not only in the button label.
- The component returns focus to the trigger on close, so the reader resumes
  where they left off. Do not move focus yourself.
- Prefer the side variant on narrow screens when the reader is deciding about
  content the centered card would cover.

### Content guidelines

- Make the title a short statement of the task: "Rename workspace", not "Are you
  sure?".
- Put the consequence in the description, in one sentence, before the actions.
- Name the action in the confirming button: "Delete project", not "Confirm".
