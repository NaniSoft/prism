## Overview

A Drawer is a panel anchored to an edge of the viewport that the reader can push
away. It ships as `Drawer`, `DrawerTrigger`, `DrawerContent`, `DrawerHeader`,
`DrawerBody`, `DrawerFooter`, `DrawerTitle`, `DrawerDescription`, `DrawerHandle`
and `DrawerClose`.

The distinction from a Sheet is a gesture and nothing else, and it is worth
stating plainly because the two read as synonyms. A `Sheet` is `Dialog` with a
named edge: the same focus trap, the same Escape key, the same outside press, the
same portal, and a panel the reader leaves by pressing something. A Drawer is a
panel the reader can also leave by dragging it off the edge with their thumb, and
it says so: it draws a grip, its exit follows the pointer, and its own content
region is marked so that selecting text with a mouse does not read as the start
of a drag. Base UI, which supplies both, draws the same line.

**If the reader can throw it away, use a Drawer. If the reader dismisses it, use
a Sheet.**

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  Drawer,
  DrawerBody,
  DrawerClose,
  DrawerContent,
  DrawerDescription,
  DrawerFooter,
  DrawerHeader,
  DrawerTitle,
  DrawerTrigger,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/drawer'

<Drawer>
  <DrawerTrigger>Adjust filters</DrawerTrigger>
  <DrawerContent side="bottom" closeLabel="Close the filters">
    <DrawerHeader>
      <DrawerTitle>Adjust filters</DrawerTitle>
      <DrawerDescription>Narrow the table to the rows you need.</DrawerDescription>
    </DrawerHeader>

    <DrawerBody>{/* the controls */}</DrawerBody>

    <DrawerFooter>
      <DrawerClose>Clear</DrawerClose>
      <Button onClick={apply}>Apply</Button>
    </DrawerFooter>
  </DrawerContent>
</Drawer>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Drawer for a focused task the reader reaches for on a phone or a tablet: a
filter set, a quick edit, a confirmation of one value, a sheet of actions on a
row. The reader arrived by tapping something and will leave by throwing the panel
away, and a thumb is the input they have.

Use it when the task is the reason they are here. A panel the reader can dismiss
is a panel they may dismiss by accident, so what is in it has to be worth the
moment of attention it costs.

### When not to use

Do not use a Drawer for anything the reader can throw away before they have read
it. A destructive decision is an `AlertDialog`, which refuses a stray press; a
short form in the middle of the page is a `Dialog`; a panel that belongs to the
page behind it is a `Sheet`.

Do not use a Drawer for a page. A full-screen bottom panel on a wide viewport is
a route, and a route is addressable, linkable and reachable by the back button.

Do not reach for a Drawer to get the gesture without wanting the gesture's other
half. Snap points and the nested-drawer stack are not exposed here, and the
reason is in the Component's documentation.

### Best practices

- Always render a `DrawerTitle`. It names the panel for every reader, and Base UI
  points the panel's `aria-labelledby` at it.
- Name the side deliberately. `bottom` for a task on a phone, `right` for a panel
  beside a list on a tablet.
- Keep the body scrollable and short. `DrawerBody` is the only part that scrolls,
  and the header and footer stay put above and below it, so the reader always
  knows what the panel is and can always reach the control that commits the task.
- Put the primary action first in `DrawerFooter`. It lands at the bottom on a
  narrow screen and at the right from `sm` up, so it is under the same thumb
  either way.
- Keep the panel's content reachable without the gesture. Escape closes it and
  the close control is on by default, because the grip is decorative and a
  decorative bar is not a way out for a reader who cannot drag.

### Accessibility

`modal` defaults to `true`, so focus is trapped inside the panel, page scroll is
locked, and the rest of the document is inert to the pointer. Focus moves into
the panel on open and returns to whatever opened it on close. Escape closes the
panel, and there is deliberately no prop that stops it: a panel a reader cannot
leave by the keyboard is a trap in both the ordinary and the technical sense.

The grip is `aria-hidden`. It is a grey bar with no name and no role, and the
reader who can use it learns what it does from the panel moving under their
thumb. The reader who cannot drag it is owed an announced control, and that is
the close control.

The close control draws its focus ring at full strength, and so does the
trigger. The panel itself draws one on `focus-visible` rather than suppressing
the indicator, because a reader who lands on the panel is on a surface nothing
else marks.

### What this is not

There are no snap points, so a half-expanded bottom sheet is not available here.
There is no nested-drawer stack and no indent effect, so a drawer inside a drawer
is not a shape this Component draws. There is no top edge, because a panel that
arrives from above the reader is a shade rather than a task surface.

### Motion

The panel's entrance is a transform across the whole width of the viewport, which
is spatial movement, and it lasts `duration-slow`, the token DESIGN.md reserves
for transform and layout state such as a disclosure. A reader who has asked for
reduced motion gets the panel already open, with no fade either, because the
panel's resting state is its full form. Nothing here carries a `motion-safe:`
guard, and neither does anything else in this package: the library's one
stylesheet ends with an unlayered `prefers-reduced-motion` rule that stops every
transition, so the decision is made once rather than at each call site.

The release from a fast flick is not matched to the flick's velocity: the panel
completes over the same 280ms whether it was thrown or nudged. Matching it would
mean a computed millisecond value in a class string, which the motion law does
not allow.

### Content guidelines

- Make the title the thing the panel is for: "Adjust filters", "Rename run",
  "Notifications".
- One sentence of description, saying what the panel changes about the page
  behind it, or what putting it away would lose.
- Keep the body to the task. A Drawer is a panel the reader may throw away, and
  every paragraph in it is a reason to read it first and dismiss it after.

It is a client component, because a drag is a stream of pointer events and a
portal needs the document.
