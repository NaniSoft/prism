## Overview

A Collapsible is one section that shows and hides, in place. It ships as
`Collapsible`, `CollapsibleTrigger` and `CollapsibleContent`.

A Collapsible is one section; an Accordion is a stack of them. The difference is
what a reader can do with the result. An accordion lets one section open by
closing another, which is right for a set of peers and wrong for a page that
needs two things visible at once. A collapsible keeps its own state and touches
nothing else, so a reader can have three open, which is usually what a page of
settings wants and an accordion forbids.

The trigger and the region are wired by the Component rather than by the caller.
A consumer who writes the two as siblings with their own `aria-expanded` gets a
button that says "collapsed" while the panel is open, or a panel no button
controls, and the failure is invisible until a reader meets it. Here the trigger
is a real button carrying the expanded state and pointing at the panel, so Enter
and Space open it and the announcement is right without the caller writing a line
of it.

A reader who collapses something must be able to find it again. The trigger stays
in the document and keeps its own words, so the way back never depends on the
panel that was just closed.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  Collapsible,
  CollapsibleContent,
  CollapsibleTrigger,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/collapsible'

<Collapsible id="billing">
  <CollapsibleTrigger>Advanced billing</CollapsibleTrigger>
  <CollapsibleContent>
    Usage is billed per seat, at the end of the month.
  </CollapsibleContent>
</Collapsible>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Collapsible for a section a reader may want out of the way and may want
back: advanced settings, a long detail, an optional explanation. It is also the
right shape when several sections on the page are independent, because nothing
here closes anything else.

### When not to use

Do not use a Collapsible to hide the primary content of a page. If a section is
essential, it should be open, not behind a click.

Do not use it when the reader needs two sections at once. That is a page, laid
out in two columns, and a reader comparing the two cannot do it with either one
closed.

Do not use an Accordion for one section, and do not use a Collapsible for a set
of peers where only one may be open. The Accordion is the one that owns that
rule.

### Best practices

- Give the section an `id`. It is the name a consumer styles from and asserts
  against, and a generated one is a name nobody else can reach.
- Put the words that name the section on the trigger. A trigger labelled
  "Advanced" is a heading, not a disclosure, and the reader has to open it to
  learn what is in it.
- Open the section the reader most likely wants, or none at all. Opening all of
  them restores the wall of text the section was meant to break up.
- Pass `hiddenUntilFound` on the content when the closed section holds prose a
  reader may be searching for. A panel that unmounts cannot be found by the
  browser's own find-in-page, and the reader who searched for a word in it is
  told the word is not on the page.
- Keep the chevron. It is the only thing that shows the section is a section, and
  it turns on the expanded state rather than on a timer.
- Keep each section's own heading level consistent across the page. The trigger
  is a button, not a heading, so the page's outline does not change when a
  section opens.

### Content guidelines

- Name the section for what is inside it: "Advanced billing", "Show raw events",
  "Keyboard shortcuts". Not "More" and not "Click to expand".
- Keep the trigger to one line. It is the only thing a reader sees when the
  section is closed, and a trigger that wraps is a trigger nobody scans.
- Put the disclosure at the end of the reasoning, not the start. A section a
  reader has to read before deciding to read it has lost them.
