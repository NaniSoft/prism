## Overview

A ButtonGroup is a set of related controls joined as one, with the focus ring drawn
once around the group rather than once per button. That is the reason it is a
Component and not a flex row: three buttons in a row each drawing their own ring
means a reader tabbing along the row watches three indicators march across a control
that reads as one thing.

## Usage

```tsx
import { ButtonGroup } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/button-group'
import { Button } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/button'

<ButtonGroup label="Text alignment">
  <Button variant="outline">Left</Button>
  <Button variant="outline">Centre</Button>
  <Button variant="outline">Right</Button>
</ButtonGroup>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a ButtonGroup for controls that operate on the same thing and that a reader
reaches together: an alignment set, an undo and redo pair, a zoom stepper, a view
switcher, a set of mutually exclusive views.

The signal is that the controls are alternatives or steps within one operation, so
moving between them is not moving between tasks. A row of three unrelated actions
beside a form is a row of buttons.

### When not to use

Do not use one for actions that are not alternatives. Save, Cancel and Duplicate are
three commands, and joining them says they are one. Put them in a `DialogFooter` or
a toolbar instead.

Do not use one for a row of links. A joined row of anchors reads as tabs, and links
that navigate are not tabs.

Do not use one where the members are a form's submit row. A primary action among
secondary ones is a hierarchy, and a joined group has one frame, which is a
contradiction.

Do not use one for more than about five members. A joined row of seven stops being
readable as a set and the ring around it stops meaning "these are one thing",
because the group is now wider than it is tall.

### Best practices

- Always pass `label`. A row of three buttons is announced as three buttons and
  nothing else, and on a page with four such rows a reader who tabs into one cannot
  tell which they are in.
- Compose `Button variant="outline"` inside it. The group draws the frame and
  removes the members' own borders, and an outline button is the one whose border is
  safe to remove; a `default` button inside a group has no border to collapse, so
  the join is a gap.
- Keep the members the same size. The ring is drawn around the group's bounds, and
  a group of three different heights has a frame that does not fit what is inside
  it.
- Do not put a disabled member at an end and expect the ring to shrink; the frame is
  the frame. Reach for a different composition instead.
- Use `orientation="vertical"` for a set that belongs beside a field rather than
  under it, and pass it, because the group states its orientation and the arrow keys
  and the geometry have to agree with it.

### Accessibility

**The ring is drawn on the group, once, and the members drop their own.** The cost is
stated rather than hidden: a ring drawn around a group is on the group, so it is
visible whether the focus arrived from the keyboard or from a click. That is a real
trade and it is the right one here, because a group of mutually exclusive or
sequential actions is nearly always operated from the keyboard, and the ambiguity of
a ring on a click is much cheaper than three rings marching. A caller who needs the
per-button indicator back composes a `Toolbar`, which is a different Component with
a different claim.

The group is a `group` with a name, not a `toolbar`. A toolbar is a set of controls
that operates on a document, with its own arrow-key model, and a ButtonGroup is
usually a set of related actions rather than an editing surface. `group` says "these
belong together" and stops there, which is the claim being made here.

Every member is still a tab stop in the document's order. A joined group is not a
composite widget: the composite model belongs to a `Toolbar` and a `RadioGroup`, and
borrowing it would take away a reader's ability to reach the third button directly.
