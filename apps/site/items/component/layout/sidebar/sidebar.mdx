## Overview

A Sidebar is a navigation rail: its own surface, its own ink, its own focus ring,
its own border, and a collapsed state that is the same rail at a smaller width
rather than a second Component.

It exists because `sidebar` through `sidebar-ring` were the one family in the token
contract with no consumer. Those tokens are not interchangeable with the page
roles. `ring` is pinned to a step that clears 3:1 against the page ground, and the
sidebar ground is one step off the page ground in either mode, so on Mint in light
mode the page ring measures 2.86:1 against the rail where `sidebar-ring` measures
4.11:1. A rail that inherited the page ring shipped a focus indicator a reader
could not see in one pack out of six, and it looked correct in the other five.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  Sidebar,
  SidebarFooter,
  SidebarItem,
  SidebarNav,
  SidebarToggle,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/sidebar'

;<Sidebar label="Sections">
  <SidebarNav label="Sections">
    <SidebarItem label="Overview" href="/overview" current />
    <SidebarItem label="Billing" href="/billing" />
    <SidebarItem label="New project" onClick={create} />
  </SidebarNav>
  <SidebarFooter>
    <SidebarToggle collapseLabel="Collapse sidebar" expandLabel="Expand sidebar" />
  </SidebarFooter>
</Sidebar>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Sidebar for the primary navigation of an application surface: a product with
more than about six destinations, a dashboard, an admin tool, anything where the
reader is moving between sections rather than reading down a page.

Compose it from `SidebarHeader` for the brand, `SidebarNav` for the links,
`SidebarFooter` for the account menu, and `SidebarToggle` wherever the product
wants the control. The toggle is a part rather than something the rail draws,
because where a rail's controls belong is the product's decision.

Use it over a top bar when the destinations are sections rather than actions, and
over a plain list of links when there are enough of them that the reader should be
able to collapse them.

### When not to use

Do not use it on a marketing page. A header with five links is a header, and a rail
on a landing page is furniture a reader has to look past.

Do not use it for a rail of icons alone. That is the collapsed state, and it is a
state of this Component rather than a different one, so an icon-only rail is
`collapsed` and a reader who lands on it can still hear every name.

Do not use one `SidebarNav` for two different sets of links. The navigation is a
landmark, and a landmark with no name is announced as "navigation" whatever is in
it, so pass a `label` for each one.

### Best practices

- **Give every item a `label`.** It is the words beside the icon while the rail is
  open and the item's accessible name while it is closed, because the words move to
  `sr-only` rather than leaving the document. An icon is not a name, and a rail
  that unmounted its labels would be a rail whose links a reader hears as
  unlabelled links.
- **Mark the current item with `current`, not with a colour.** It sets
  `aria-current="page"`, which is what a reader is told and what a browser's
  find-in-page uses to find the current page, and it draws a rail down the item's
  leading edge, which is a shape. Colour is the third signal and never the only
  one.
- **Control the state when it has to survive a reload.** Pass `collapsed` with
  `onCollapsedChange` and keep it. A product that forgets the rail on every page
  load teaches a reader that collapsing it was a mistake.
- **Put the count on the page, not in the rail.** A trailing slot is `aria-hidden`
  and is not drawn while the rail is collapsed, so an item's name is its `label` in
  both states. A count a reader must have is the count where they land.
- **Use an item with no `href` for something that acts, and give it an
  `onClick`.** It renders a real button, because an anchor with no `href` is not a
  link and a keyboard cannot reach it. The handler is a prop for the same reason
  the label is: a button a reader can find and press with no result is the same
  defect as a list that renders an empty item.

### Accessibility

The rail is a `div` and carries no name, because a `div` has no role and an
`aria-label` on one is a label nothing reads. The landmark is `SidebarNav`, and it
is named there. The toggle carries `aria-expanded` and points `aria-controls` at
the rail by id, so the region it controls is the element the rail actually
rendered.

Both states of the rail are the same rail. The hover surface, the focus ring, the
current rail and the accessible name are all present when the rail is collapsed as
well as when it is open, and only the words and the padding change. That is the
claim worth holding: an icon-only rail that dropped any of the four would be a
worse version of the thing rather than a smaller one.

The focus ring is drawn in `sidebar-ring` at full strength, which is the role
measured against this surface rather than the page's. It is drawn *in addition to*
the browser's own outline rather than in place of it. Every other focusable
Component in Prism suppresses the outline and draws `ring-ring` instead; this one
does not, because `check-focus-indicators.mjs` recognises exactly one ring role in
the pattern it reads a full-strength ring from, and a Component that claimed to
replace the outline here would be claiming a replacement the repository cannot
verify.

### Measured, not assumed

`sidebar-ring` on `sidebar` is 3.78:1 in the base pack's dark mode, which is the
tightest required pair in that mode in the whole contrast table. It clears 3:1, but
with 0.78 of headroom rather than the 1.7 the page ring has on the page ground.

The pair the table does not measure is the one to know about: `sidebar-ring`
against `sidebar-accent`, the surface a focused item sits on while it is also
hovered or selected, is 3.19:1 in the base pack's dark mode and 3.82:1 to 4.21:1
in the light packs. It passes everywhere, and it is the tightest unmeasured pair
in this family.
