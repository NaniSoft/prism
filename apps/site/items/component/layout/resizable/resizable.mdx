## Overview

A Resizable is two panes with a divider between them that both a pointer and a
keyboard move. It ships as `Resizable`, `ResizablePanel` and `ResizableHandle`.

The divider is the Component, and everything about it is a decision a consumer
would otherwise make badly. It is an ARIA separator that is focusable, so the
split is operable without a pointer at all, and it reports `aria-valuenow` as the
position along the split, so a reader is told where the divider is rather than
only being able to feel it. Arrow keys move it by a small step, Home takes it to
one end and End to the other, and both are clamped, so a reader who holds the key
stops at the end rather than pushing a pane to nothing and being unable to get it
back.

It remembers nothing across a reload, and that is deliberate. The position is a
prop and a callback, so a caller who wants a reader's split to survive a reload
persists it and passes it back. A design system that wrote to a reader's storage
unasked would be making a claim about a product it does not know: which pane this
is, whether a second reader of the same account wants the same number, and
whether this reader wants it remembered at all.

Both the group and the divider require a `label`, and there is no default for
either. A resizable is announced as two panes with a divider between them, so two
unnamed panes are a page where a reader is told "region" twice; and a divider
with no name is announced as "separator", so a page with three of them is a page
where a screen reader user cannot tell which one they are about to move.

## Usage

```tsx
import {
  Resizable,
  ResizableHandle,
  ResizablePanel,
} from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/resizable'

<Resizable label="Runs and details" defaultPosition={40} onPositionChange={save}>
  <ResizablePanel size={40}>Run list</ResizablePanel>
  <ResizableHandle label="Resize the run list" />
  <ResizablePanel size={60}>Run detail</ResizablePanel>
</Resizable>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Resizable where the right amount of space is a matter of what the reader is
doing: a file tree beside an editor, an inbox beside a message, a log beside its
details. The reader is the only one who knows how wide their work needs to be.

### When not to use

Do not use a Resizable where the panes have a known right size, such as a form
and a submit button. A fixed grid is one less thing to get wrong, and a divider
a reader can drag to nothing is worse than no divider.

Do not use it for a layout that has to survive a reload at a known proportion,
such as a print view or an export. A reader who drags a pane and comes back to a
reset split cannot take a screenshot of the layout they meant.

Do not use it on a phone. A divider a reader can drag with a finger on a four
hundred pixel screen is a divider they will drag by accident, and a two-pane
layout does not fit there anyway.

### Best practices

- Give both panes a real `size`, and keep them summing to a hundred. A pane left
  at its default beside a sized one is a split nobody chose.
- Persist the position yourself. Read `onPositionChange`, store the number
  wherever your product keeps a preference, and pass it back as `position` or
  `defaultPosition`. The Component stores nothing.
- Name each divider for the pane it moves: "Resize the run list". That is the
  name a screen reader user hears while holding it.
- Give the group a `label` naming what the two panes are together, not what one
  of them is.
- Give the group a height when `orientation` is vertical, and a width when it is
  horizontal. The group fills its parent, and a parent with no size is a
  divider with nothing to divide.
- Set `disabled` on the group while a layout is loading, rather than hiding the
  divider and shifting the panes underneath the reader.
- Expect a drag to be measured against the group's size at the moment of the press. A
  group that changes size while a pointer is down on the divider finishes that drag
  against the travel it started with; end the drag and start it again rather than
  resizing the group under the reader's hand.

### Content guidelines

- Put the primary content in the second pane and the navigation in the first, so
  the divider moving right makes the work wider and moving left makes the list
  narrower, which is what a reader expects.
- Do not put a destructive control on the divider. It is a drag target, and a
  drag that ends on a button is a click the reader did not mean.
- Keep each pane scrollable on its own. A group that scrolls as one piece makes
  the divider a way to lose a reader's place in both panes at once.
