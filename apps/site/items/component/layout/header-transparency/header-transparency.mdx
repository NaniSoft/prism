## Overview

HeaderTransparency is the scroll state that makes a page bar invisible at the
top of the page and translucent below it. Rendered once, it writes two
attributes and nothing else: `data-header="transparent"` on the document, so the
stylesheet knows the page wants a transparent bar, and `data-scrolled` on the
bar itself once the reader is past twelve pixels of scroll.

The transparency itself is CSS, in the package stylesheet, keyed on those two
attributes: at the top the bar carries no surface and no hairline, so the brand
and the navigation are bare text over the page's ground, and below it the bar
gains a translucent, frosted surface and its hairline, so content passing beneath
it never reads as if it runs into the labels.

## Usage

```tsx
import { HeaderTransparency } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/header-transparency'

<HeaderTransparency />
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it on a page whose first screen is a hero that fills the viewport, where the
bar would otherwise draw an opaque band over the page's own ground. The bar
keeps its own opaque surface wherever this component is not rendered, so the
about, docs and blog pages of a site are untouched by it.

### When not to use

Do not use it on a page that has no tall first screen: a bar that is transparent
over a paragraph is a bar whose labels have nothing behind them. And do not
render it twice on one page.

### Best practices

- Render one, above or beside the bar it describes, and let the stylesheet carry
  the look.
- Leave it under reduced motion. The change of state is not a movement, so the
  package's universal rule makes it an instant one rather than removing it.
