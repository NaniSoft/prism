## Overview

An Announcement is a page-level message bar, with an optional action and an
optional dismiss. It ships `Announcement` and `AnnouncementTone`, and the five
tones are the five the semantic contract publishes.

**Every tone states its own fill and its own ink, and the reason is a measured
failure rather than a preference.** An announcement is body text sitting on a
coloured surface, so an inherited ink is not the page's foreground by accident: it
is whatever happened to be behind the viewport, and the failure `Cta01` measured
was 1.01:1 on exactly that arrangement. A tone that set only a fill would be the
same bar in a different colour, and no token gate could see it, because the gate
measures token pairs rather than what a Component leaves out.

**The order is message, then action, then dismiss, and the order is a decision
about where the eye goes.** The message comes first because it is the reason the
bar exists. The action comes second because it is what to do about the message. The
dismiss comes last because it is the only control in the bar that is about the bar
rather than about the message, and a reader who reaches for it has already read
the thing.

**A dismissed bar hides itself, and Prism does not persist that.** The state is
local, so a reload brings the message back, which is the honest behaviour for a
notice about something still true. A stored dismissal is a record in a consumer's
storage that says this reader has already been told, and it outlives the message.
When it expires, whether it is scoped to a message rather than a page, and whether
a reader who changes a setting should be told again, are decisions about the
consumer's product. `onDismiss` is the seam: the bar hides itself, and the caller
owns the half that outlives the page.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Announcement } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/announcement'

<Announcement
  tone="warning"
  message="Two collectors stopped reporting."
  action={<Button size="sm" variant="outline">See which</Button>}
  dismissible
  dismissLabel="Dismiss the collector notice"
  onDismiss={remember}
>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use an Announcement for something true about the page right now that a reader
would otherwise not learn: a background job finished, a setting took effect, a
plan changed, a feature is temporarily degraded.

Use it when the message is about the page rather than about one control. A failure
beside the field that caused it is an `Alert`; a failure that blocks is a `Dialog`;
a fact the reader did not have to be told is a `Toast`.

### When not to use

Do not use an Announcement for something the reader caused and must act on. A
message that requires an action cannot sit in a bar a reader scrolls past, and the
control that fixes it has to be somewhere the reader already is.

Do not use it for content that is not transient. If the sentence is true next week
too, it is a section of the page, not a bar above it.

Do not use `destructive` for a mistake the reader made. It is for a failure they
did not cause and would not otherwise learn: a payment declined on the server, a
session that expired, a deploy that failed. A mistake belongs beside the control
where the reader can see what to change.

### Best practices

- Keep the message to one sentence, or to a short block that a reader can read
  without scrolling. The bar is a fixed band; a message that wraps to four lines
  is a section wearing a bar's clothes.
- Make the action a real link or a real button of your own. A bar at the top of a
  page is the wrong place for the only route to the thing it is about, and a
  reader who missed the bar cannot take an action that only existed inside it.
- Use `info` for a notice and `warning` for something that will cost the reader
  something if they ignore it. `info` wears the pack's own hue because it is the
  strongest ink the contract offers on a coloured surface, and a notification is
  not a decoration.
- Keep the dismiss label specific. "Dismiss" is a word a reader has to interpret
  against which of five bars it is, and four consumer products in two languages
  cannot all be given "Close".
- Keep the action to one word on a narrow screen. The bar holds three parts on one
  line and the action is the one that gives way.

### Accessibility

The bar is a `role="status"` and not a `role="alert"`, and the difference is
whether the reader is interrupted. An announcement is in the document rather than
arriving into it: it is there on first paint, a reader can find it by browsing,
and it is not a consequence of the thing they were doing a moment ago. An alert is
assertive, which cuts across whatever the reader was reading, and that is right
for a failure the reader caused and has to fix. The cost of `status` is real: a
reader who arrives mid-page and never looks up misses the bar entirely, and the
fix is where you place it, not a louder role.

The dismiss control is required to be named whenever the bar can be dismissed, and
the type makes that a requirement in that shape rather than a default. An
icon-only control a reader reaches towards has to say what it is before they press
it.

The action and the dismiss are the last two tab stops in the bar, in that order,
and the bar takes no focus itself. Nothing here calls `focus()`, so a reader who
is typing is not interrupted by a notice arriving.
