## Overview

A LiveRegion is a region that announces what just changed in it. It renders
exactly the content it is given and carries the politeness and the busy state a
screen reader reads, so a result list that filters as you type, a search whose
count updates, and a stream that appends to the page are all audible rather than
silent.

It renders nothing at all when it has nothing to say. A live region that is always
present announces every unrelated state change of its ancestors, so the empty case
is the absence of the element rather than an empty element.

It owns the region and not the transport. The socket, the retry, the persistence
and the order events arrive in belong to the consumer.

## Usage

```tsx
import { LiveRegion } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/live-region'

<LiveRegion label="Run output" busy={streaming}>
  {lines.map((line) => (
    <p key={line.id}>{line.text}</p>
  ))}
</LiveRegion>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a LiveRegion when content arrives without the reader having asked for it:
a filtered result count, a log that grows, a background job that finished. The
region is what makes the change audible.

Use it around a region that is loading in place, rather than only where the
change is most important. A reader who hears one announcement and misses the
next two has been told less than a reader who hears all three at a lower
interruptiveness.

### When not to use

Do not use a LiveRegion for content the reader asked for and is already looking
at. Opening a dialog and hearing its contents announced is a reader being told
what they just did. A Dialog owns its own announcement.

Do not use one as a wrapper for the whole page. A permanently present live
region announces every state change of its ancestors, which is the noise this
Component exists to avoid as well as to cause.

Do not use `politeness="assertive"` to make something feel more responsive. It
does not make it arrive sooner, it makes it interrupt a screen reader
mid-sentence. Reserve it for a change the reader is genuinely blocked on.

### Best practices

- Set `busy` while more is expected and clear it when the last item lands. A
  caller that sets it permanently has told assistive technology the stream never
  ends, which is a claim about the future that is not usually true.
- Put the region around the region that changes, not around the page. A region
  that wraps a list and announces its own length is announcing a number a reader
  cannot use.
- Pass a `label` when the arrival would otherwise be unplaceable. A run whose
  messages are self-describing does not need one; a region announcing bare
  status does, because "running" alone says nothing about what.
- Keep the visible content and the announced content the same thing. A region
  that announces a summary of what it renders is two sources of truth, and they
  drift.
- Do not put focusable content in the region. A live region is announced, not
  entered, and a focusable child inside one competes with the announcement for
  the reader's attention.

### Content guidelines

The words are the consumer's. A shared library cannot know whether a status is
"Payment declined" or "Seu pagamento foi recusado", and a default that guesses is
a sentence a reader hears in a language they did not choose.
