## Overview

A Spinner says work is happening and nothing about how much of it is left. It is
a ring, it names itself, and it is a status rather than a control: a reader is
told what is busy and the page carries on being usable.

**It is not a Progress and it is not a Skeleton, and the three are confused
often enough to be worth stating.** A Skeleton stands in for content: it has
the shape of the thing that is arriving, so the page does not move when the
content lands. A Progress reports a position: it carries a value, a minimum and
a maximum, so a screen reader is told "68 percent" rather than the width of a
coloured bar. A Spinner carries neither a shape nor a value. Pick by the
question: *what will arrive* is a Skeleton, *how far along* is a Progress, and
*is anything happening at all* is a Spinner.

**The honest reason a Spinner is often the wrong answer is that it causes a
layout shift.** The ring is a fixed square, so the content it stands in for is
not, and everything below it moves twice: once when the content is replaced by
the ring, and again when the ring is replaced by the content. A reader's eye
has to find the thing twice. That is the whole cost of this Component, and it
is not paid when the shape of what is coming is already known.

The `label` is required and there is no default. A busy indicator with no name
is an animation, and an animation is nothing to a reader who cannot see it. The
words are the consumer's, because a shared library cannot know whether the
answer is "Loading invoices" or "Carregando faturas". The ring itself is
`aria-hidden`, so a reader is told what is busy exactly once.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Spinner } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/spinner'

<Spinner label="Loading invoices" />
<Spinner label="Saving" size="sm" />
<Spinner label="Preparing your workspace" size="lg" />
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Spinner when something is happening and the reader cannot be told how
much of it is left: a filter that has not come back yet, a panel whose contents
are being fetched for the first time, a control that is waiting on a request
whose size is unknown.

Use it beside the words rather than instead of them. The ring is the
confirmation that something is happening; the sentence beside it is what a
reader can act on, and in most of the cases where a Spinner is right there are
already words on the page saying what is being waited for.

### When not to use

Do not use a Spinner as a page-level loading state where the layout is known.
A first paint of Skeletons is a better answer, because a Skeleton holds the
page still. A Spinner alone in the middle of an empty page tells the reader
nothing about the page they are about to get.

Do not use a Spinner for a task of known length. A countable upload is a
`Progress`; a bar that shows real information beats a ring that shows anxiety.

Do not use a Skeleton where you mean a Spinner, or the reverse. A Skeleton is
for content whose shape you know. A Spinner is for work whose extent you do
not.

Do not use a Spinner for a wait under about a second. A control that disables
itself for 400ms and then re-enables is a control that flickers.

Do not put a Spinner inside a Button beside a label that already says what is
happening. The label is the announcement; the ring beside it is a second copy
of a sentence the reader can already see.

### Best practices

- Name the work, not the Component. "Loading invoices" beats "Loading", and a
  page with two spinners needs the same distinction a sighted reader gets from
  seeing which panel each is in.
- Keep the size to the three authored steps. `className` is layout only on
  every Component in this package, and a ring sized by a layout decision is a
  ring that can be invisible.
- Use one Spinner per region. Two rings in one panel is a reader being told
  twice that neither has finished.
- Turn the Spinner off when the work is done, and remove it rather than leaving
  it at zero. A ring that has stopped meaning anything is a picture.
- Mark the *region* as busy while more is expected, not the ring. That is what
  `LiveRegion`'s `busy` prop is for, and a `busy` flag left up permanently has
  told assistive technology a stream never ends.

### Content guidelines

- Write the label in the present continuous and name the thing: "Saving",
  "Loading invoices", "Connecting". A past-tense label on a spinner that is
  still running is a sentence that contradicts the ring.
- Keep it to a few words. A screen reader reads it in full, and so does
  everyone using find-in-page.
- Do not use the label to carry an error. A failure is not a busy state; put it
  in an `Alert` where the reader is looking and can act on it.
