## Overview

A Status is a dot in one of five tones and the caller's words beside it. It is the
smallest way a product says what state something is in without spending a badge
on it.

The decision that shapes it is that the tone is required. A Component can see the
state and it cannot see whether the state is urgent, so a tone is a judgement the
caller makes, for exactly the reason `Meter` holds its thresholds at arm's length.
A default would be the quietest way to ship a claim, because it looks like a
convenience and behaves like an opinion.

The label is required for the second reason: a status with no words is a colour a
screen reader cannot read and a reader who cannot see colour cannot name.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Status } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/status'

<Status tone="success" label="All systems operational" />

<Status tone="warning" size="sm" label="2 of 3 replicas lagging" />
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Status where a row, a list item or a heading needs a state in words, and
where the state is a word first and a colour second. A deploy state, a region, a
plan, a queue, a component's health.

Use `size="sm"` in a dense row where an `md` dot competes with the numbers beside
it, and the default `md` beside a heading or in a summary card.

Use `neutral` for a state with nothing to alarm about. It is drawn in the muted
foreground rather than the full ink, because a dot in the foreground colour reads
as an emphasis mark rather than as a state.

### When not to use

Do not use a Status for the whole of a state. The dot is a redundant channel; the
label is the meaning. A row that is only a dot is a picture a reader has to
decode, and a screen reader announces nothing at all.

Do not use one where a Badge belongs. A Badge is a compact label with a surface,
for a category or a tier the reader is meant to read as a chip. A Status is a dot
plus a word, and it takes up a fraction of the row.

Do not reach for `info` to mean "not urgent". `info` is a state in its own right,
and a product whose states are neutral, info, success, warning and destructive has
five states. A product with three states passes those three.

Do not add a sixth tone because a product has a sixth state. A wider list is a
wider set of colours a reader has to learn and a maintainer has to keep true, and
the words are what make each tone legible. If the sixth state is genuinely
different, request it upstream rather than reaching for the nearest tone.

### Best practices

- Pass the tone you mean rather than the tone that is easiest. The list is
  `neutral`, `info`, `success`, `warning` and `destructive`, and nothing is
  derived from the label.
- Write the label in the product's own vocabulary. A product that says "Degraded"
  and a product that says "Partially unavailable" are both describing `warning`,
  and neither word is Prism's to choose.
- The dot is `aria-hidden` and the label carries the meaning, so a reader who
  cannot separate the tones still reads which is which. Do not rely on the dot to
  say something the label does not.
- `warning` is amber and measures about 2.1:1 against the default pack's light
  background, so on a light surface that one dot is a tint rather than a mark. The
  label beside it is what carries the state.
