## Overview

A Price is a monetary amount with its currency, optionally what it replaced and
optionally what it is per. It is the one Component in this system that hands a
number to the platform rather than printing it itself.

The decision that shapes it is `Intl`. A library that hardcoded a dollar sign and
two decimal places would be making two claims about every consumer that installed
it: that the consumer's market is the one that writes dollars, and that the
consumer's prices have two decimal places of meaning. Both are wrong somewhere in
the world. `Intl` is the platform's own answer, and it is already localised.

The second decision is that a comparison is a strikethrough rather than a Badge,
because a strikethrough is the convention every reader already knows, and a Badge
would put a filled surface and a colour on a claim about a price the consumer
owns.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Price } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/price'

<Price amount={19} currency="USD" locale="en-US" compareAt={29} period="/mo" />

<Price
  amount={19}
  format={(amount) => `${new Intl.NumberFormat('de-DE', {
    style: 'currency',
    currency: 'EUR',
    maximumFractionDigits: 0,
  }).format(amount)}`}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Price wherever a product states what something costs: a plan, a seat, a
credit, a line item, a total. It is the Component for a figure whose unit is a
currency, because a currency is a machine value a platform recognises rather than
a sentence anyone has to translate.

Pass `locale` on a page that serves more than one language. Omitted, the runtime's
own default is used, which is the right answer on a server and the wrong one on a
multilingual page.

Pass `maximumFractionDigits` when the price has no meaningful fraction. A whole
number of seats with `.00` on the end of every row is a price nobody believes.

Use `compareAt` for the amount this one replaced, and `period` for what the amount
is per, in the product's own words.

### When not to use

Do not use a Price for a figure that is not money. A token count, a quota and a
duration are numbers with units, and a currency sign on any of them is a claim
about what they are.

Do not use it for a range or a "from" price. A formatter that has to assemble two
amounts is a formatter, and passing one through `format` is how you do that here
rather than a variant prop.

Do not rely on a Price to hold a layout open while its amount loads. With no
`amount` it renders nothing, which is the honest answer, and the consequence is
that the row closes up and reopens. Reserve the space in the surrounding layout
if the jump matters.

Do not reach for a Badge to mark a discount. A Badge is a compact label with a
surface. A comparison is a strikethrough.

### Best practices

- With no `amount`, a Price renders an empty fragment. That is deliberate: a dash
  or a zero would claim the price is zero, which is a claim about a consumer's
  revenue that no reader can disprove.
- The period sits with the amount, before the comparison, so a struck-through
  price never reads as another per-period figure.
- `format` replaces the platform's formatter completely rather than adjusting it,
  and the type is a union for that reason: a caller who passes `format` is not
  also made to invent a currency code for a formatter that will never read it.
- Sizes are the four authored type-scale steps and nothing else. `xl` is the
  largest a price can be, because a price set larger than the title beside it stops
  reading as a price.
- Both amounts go through the same formatter, so the current price and the price
  it replaced are never rendered by two different rules.
