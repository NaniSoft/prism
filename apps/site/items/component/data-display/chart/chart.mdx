## Overview

A Chart draws one set of numbers: bars, a line, an area under a line, or a ring
for a part-to-whole. It takes the series, the categories along the bottom and
the name a reader will hear, and it derives the vertical axis from the data.

The decision that shapes it is that the chart is never the only way to read the
numbers. Every Chart draws a table of the same series into the document, visibly
hidden, whether or not anybody looks at it. A screen reader navigates it by row
and by cell, a crawler reads the figures out of it, and a print stylesheet loses
the colour that separated two series and still gets the values. That is the
argument `Carousel` makes about everything a carousel shows, applied to a
picture: everything a chart draws, a reader must be able to reach some other way.

The second decision is the axis. Prism derives the maximum and rounds it up to a
readable step, and it does not take a caller supplied maximum by default, because
a caller who pre-normalises has two chances to get the axis wrong and a
mislabelled axis is a lie with a typeface on it. `yMax` is there for the one case
that needs it, and it says what it costs.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Chart, type ChartSeries } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/chart'

const months = ['Jan', 'Feb', 'Mar', 'Apr', 'May', 'Jun']

const series: ChartSeries[] = [
  { name: 'API', values: [120, 180, 150, 240, 210, 300], tone: 'chart-1' },
  { name: 'Web', values: [60, 90, 140, 120, 160, 180], tone: 'chart-2' },
]

<Chart
  variant="bar"
  labels={months}
  series={series}
  label="Requests per month by surface"
  caption="April is the month the collector changed."
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Chart for a value read against a shared baseline across a handful of
categories: requests per month, signups per week, latency per region, margin per
quarter. The signal is that a reader wants the shape and one or two exact
numbers.

Use `variant="bar"` when the categories are discrete and the value is read as a
length. Use `variant="line"` when the value moves continuously and the shape
between two points is the point. Use `variant="area"` when the reader wants how
much there was rather than where it went. Use `variant="donut"` for a
part-to-whole with two parts and nothing else.

Use it over a table when there are few enough categories to read without
scrolling sideways. Past about twenty, a sortable table wins, because a reader
who wants April should not have to read five other months to find it.

### When not to use

Do not use a Chart for a single number. A KPI belongs in a Metric, and a chart
of one bar is a number with axes drawn around it.

Do not use it for a distribution, a ranking, a correlation or a histogram. This
is a linear frame with one axis, one baseline and up to five series. Those are a
charting library's work, and this Component is the frame and the table that
library's marks should sit inside rather than the library.

Do not use `variant="donut"` for a part-to-whole of three or more parts. At
three the ring stops being read as a proportion and starts being read as a
puzzle, and three bars are the honest default. The variant takes exactly two
series with one value each, or one series with a single value, and refuses
everything else.

Do not use a Chart where the reader is looking a figure up. A chart shows a
shape; a value a reader has to find is a table row.

### Best practices

- **Name the tone, or accept that the series is not a series.** `tone` is
  `chart-1` through `chart-5` and it is optional. Omitted, the series is drawn
  in the supporting ink rather than in a series role, because a colour is a claim
  about what a thing is and a Component that knows nothing about your product has
  no business making it. Naming the tone is how you say the series are meant to
  be told apart.
- **Keep the number of series to what a reader can hold.** The five roles are
  distinct from each other in every pack and both modes, and the contrast gate
  asserts exactly that, but past about three series hue is the only channel left.
  Two charts of three series beat one chart of six.
- **Pass `labels`.** It is the array that makes the chart a claim about your
  domain rather than about a design system, and it is drawn for `bar` and `line`
  and read on the table for every variant.
- **Write the caption.** The sentence under the chart is a claim about the data
  and only you know which one is true: a fall, a seasonal dip, a collection that
  changed, a filter that matched nothing.
- **Reach for `yMax` deliberately and read `data-clamped` in the markup.** A
  value above the ceiling you set is drawn clamped to the top gridline and
  counted, so a ceiling that is too low is a fact rather than a mark floating
  above its own axis.
- **Move the legend rather than stacking it.** Pass `legend={false}` and render
  `ChartLegend` where the surrounding layout wants it. The legend is drawn by
  default once there is more than one series, because it is the only statement
  of the series to colour assignment anywhere on the figure.

### Accessibility

The table is the chart's accessible representation, and it is part of the
Component rather than a note in the documentation, because a note is not a
guarantee. It is a real `<table>` with a `<caption>` naming the figure and its
series, a row head for each category and a column head for each series. The
marks and the cells are two renderings of one `series` array, so they cannot
drift, and there is no prop that turns it off.

The geometry is `aria-hidden`. A mark is a shape at a position, and announcing
forty of them would be a list of coordinates rather than a chart.

Two of the five roles measure below 3:1 against a light card, `chart-2` at
2.49:1 and `chart-3` at 2.15:1 in the default pack, so a mark in either is found
by its position and its shape rather than by its colour. That is what the legend
is for, and it is why the legend is not decoration on a printed chart either,
where every series goes to the same grey.

### What this is not

There is no chart library under it, no hover tooltip, no crosshair, no zoom, no
stacking, no curve interpolation, no second axis and no transition on any of the
geometry. A tooltip answers a question a pointer asked, which is a question a
keyboard never asks and a print stylesheet cannot receive.

A bar does not grow. The motion law refuses entrance effects, so a chart would
not need an argument to be refused, and there is no reason for one: a bar's
height is its value, so a transition between two values is a transition between
two facts, and a reader watching one arrive has learned nothing they could not
have read from the second one. This is a server Component, and the whole figure
is arithmetic over props.

### Content guidelines

- Name the figure in the caller's own words: what is being plotted, not what the
  component is. The `label` is the only name a reader hears.
- Name the series the way the product names them. A legend entry is a word a
  reader scans, not a sentence.
- Keep the caption to one sentence and give it a reason. "April is the month the
  collector changed" is a caption. "Requests" is a title.
