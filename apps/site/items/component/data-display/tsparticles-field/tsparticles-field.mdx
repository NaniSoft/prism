## Overview

TsparticlesField is a living field whose links reach out to the reader's pointer:
a connected network drawn by tsParticles over a complete static floor. Where
`ParticleField` drifts on the ambient cycle and answers the pointer with a quiet
repulsion, this one puts the reader's cursor inside the medium, and the network
grabs toward it.

The floor is the contract, and the engine only ever adds the motion. The static
field is rendered on the server, so a reader with scripting off, a crawler, a
print stylesheet, or a reader who asked the platform for less motion meets a
complete figure at rest rather than an empty rectangle.

## Usage

```tsx
import { TsparticlesField } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/tsparticles-field'

<div className="relative overflow-hidden">
  <TsparticlesField className="absolute inset-0" />
  <div className="relative">{children}</div>
</div>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where a page wants the medium to answer the cursor, for example a hero
that says the reader can reach into the system. The links are what make it a
medium rather than dust, so size `linkDistance` to the band.

It composes behind type rather than beside it, as a ground, exactly as
`ParticleField` does, and it pairs with `AuroraWash` when the claim is light
moving through the medium as well as across it.

### When not to use

Do not use it behind a reading passage, and do not use it to say something: it
is `aria-hidden` because a medium is not information.

Do not stack two media on one page, and do not load the engine where a still
figure would do. The bundle is the one real cost this component carries; a page
that only needs a ground should use `SignalField` or `ParticleField` instead.

### Best practices

- Keep the count in the dozens and the link distance near 150 pixels: a denser
  field is a haze, and the clamp will not save a composition that wanted fog.
- Trust the floor. The engine arms over markup that already is the figure, so
  the component never renders nothing until a script runs.
