## Overview

A Sparkline is a series drawn small enough to sit in a cell: a line, no axes, no
labels and no legend. It exists for the row where a number and its shape both
matter and there is no room for a chart.

The decision that shapes it is that the drawing is decorative and the numbers are
not. Ninety-six pixels of line with no axis is a shape, and a shape a reader
cannot query is the failure a chart exists to prevent, so the same array is also
rendered as a table in a visually hidden wrapper.

The second decision is that two inputs throw rather than draw. An empty series is
a frame with no claim in it, and a series whose readings are all equal is a
straight stroke with no information in it. A reader cannot tell a flat line from a
broken chart.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Sparkline } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/sparkline'

<Sparkline label="Requests per hour" values={[12, 18, 14, 22, 19, 31]} tone="chart-1" />

<Sparkline
  label="Error rate, last 30 builds"
  values={[0.4, 0.2, 0.9, 1.4, 0.3, 0.2]}
  tone="chart-5"
  emphasizeLast
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a Sparkline beside a number in a table row, a list item or a summary card,
where the number is the reading and the shape is the context. A week's requests,
a build's error rate, a queue's depth, a run's duration.

Pass raw counts. The Component scales them against the largest reading in the
series, because a caller that pre-normalises has two chances to get the axis wrong
and a mislabelled axis is a lie with a typeface on it.

Pass `emphasizeLast` when the last reading is the one the reader came for and the
shape behind it is context. Leave it off otherwise, because a dot on the end of a
line is a claim that the end is special.

### When not to use

Do not use a Sparkline where a reader has to compare values. The numbers are in a
hidden table, which means finding one is a deliberate act rather than a glance.
That is `ChartFrame`, with a visible table, a legend and an axis.

Do not use one with a series that is flat. That is not a sparkline, it is a
straight line with no information in it, and the Component throws rather than draw
it because a reader cannot tell the two apart.

Do not use one for a second series. A sparkline has one line and no legend, and
adding a second means adding the legend and the colours that go with it, which is
`ChartFrame`.

Do not use it in a cell that has to resize with its column. The box is in pixels
on purpose, so that a column of rows lines up.

### Best practices

- Give every Sparkline a `label`. It is the figure's name, the caption on its
  table and the only thing a screen reader reads before the numbers. Two
  sparklines in a column of numbers are two pictures a reader cannot tell apart.
- Take the tone from `chart-1` through `chart-5` and never from a raw colour. A
  series colour is a claim about what a thing is, and the caller makes it.
- Expect a narrow range to look dramatic. The line is scaled to the series' own
  range with no floor at zero, which is what lets a series between 900 and 1,000
  show its shape. The price is that a two percent move looks like a cliff, and the
  answer is the table, which carries the numbers at the precision you gave them.
- The hidden table is generated from the same `values` array, so it cannot fall
  behind the line. It has no column headings, because this Item has no word for a
  position that is true in your domain.
- `width` and `height` are pixels. 96 by 24 is a mark beside a number, not a plot,
  and a box tall enough to read an axis is `ChartFrame`.
