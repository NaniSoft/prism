## Overview

ParticleField is a living field: points drifting on linked paths, the medium a
page's largest statements stand in front of. It is the one script-driven figure
in the system, and the static field it renders on the server is the contract
that makes that safe: stop the loop, disarm the canvas, print the page, and the
figure is still a field, still true, still legible.

## Usage

```tsx
import { ParticleField } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/components/particle-field'

<div className="relative overflow-hidden">
  <ParticleField count={64} className="absolute inset-0" />
  <div className="relative">{children}</div>
</div>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where a page claims there is a medium to move through: a hero that says
traffic flows, a band that says an estate is observed, a ground that says the
surface is live. The links are what make it a medium rather than dust, so size
`linkDistance` to the band: a wide band carries longer lines than a panel.

Give it room behind content that has its own position and its own contrast. It
is a ground, exactly as `SignalField` is, and it composes behind type rather
than beside it.

### When not to use

Do not use it behind a reading passage. A medium that moves while a reader
reads is the first thing a reader with vestibular sensitivity will ask to have
gone, and `SignalField` still, or nothing, is the ground a passage wants.

Do not use it to say something. It is `aria-hidden` by default because a medium
is not information; a figure that carries a claim is `PulseGraph` or
`PulseSeries`, both of which require a name.

Do not stack two of them. Two media on one page is a collision, and the reader
cannot tell which one they are looking through.

### Best practices

- Leave `interactive` on wherever a reader's hand can reach the ground, and
  turn it off under text a reader will select, because a surface that moves
  during a selection is a surface fighting the selection.
- Keep the count in the dozens. A field with hundreds of points is a haze, and
  the clamp will not save a composition that wanted fog.
- Pair it with `AuroraWash` when the claim is light moving through the medium
  as well as across it, and let the two differ in tempo, which they already do
  by an order of magnitude.
- Trust the floor. The component that renders nothing until a script runs is
  the retired line's defect, and this one renders the whole figure before any
  script decides to help.
