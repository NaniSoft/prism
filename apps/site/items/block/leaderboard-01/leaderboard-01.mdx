## Overview

Leaderboard01 is a ranked set: a position, a name, an optional mark, a figure with
the caller's own sentence for it, an optional series beside it, and an optional
destination. The order is the caller's and the Block never sorts, because a
leaderboard ranked by a metric the Block cannot see is a list whose order the
caller has to fix, and a `sort` prop would be a second derivation to keep in step
with their own data.

`valueLabel` is required on every entry, and it is the sentence the figure is
announced as. "1,204 runs", "1,204 this month" and "1,204" are three different
sentences about one figure, and a bare numeral beside a name tells a screen reader
nothing about what it counts.

`emphasis` is how a caller marks their own rows, and the mark is a border and a
weight rather than a trophy, because a leaderboard is read by people who are on it.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Leaderboard01, type Leaderboard01Entry } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/leaderboard-01'

<Leaderboard01
  eyebrow="Last 30 days"
  title="Who ran the most"
  unit="runs"
  entries={ENTRIES}
  emphasis={['ada']}
  empty="No runs in the last thirty days. The first one usually takes an afternoon."
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it for a small set a reader is ranking in their head: a dozen people, twenty
projects, five machines. It is a list of names with a figure and a position, drawn
so the position can be scanned down the leading edge.

Use `rank={false}` for a set a reader is meant to read as peers, where the first
three positions would say more about the page than about the entries.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a long set. Past about twenty entries a reader is comparing rather
than ranking, which is a table, and a table is `DataTable01` with the caller's own
header for every column.

Do not use it for a set where the order is the point but you have not decided it. A
leaderboard whose order was chosen by a default rather than by a query is a claim
about who matters that nobody made.

Do not use it where the figure is money and a currency belongs in the reading. The
figure is a number and the number carries no unit, so the unit is your `unit` and
the formatted reading is your `valueLabel`.

### Best practices

- Rank in your own query and pass the result. Do not expect this Block to sort, and
  do not add a `sort` to your page around it: two orderings of one list that
  disagree is a list whose order nobody can fix.
- Write `valueLabel` as the figure at a period, and let `unit` be the noun. The row
  is announced as the noun then your sentence, so a label that already names the
  noun says it twice.
- Pass `seriesLabel` whenever you pass a `series`. Two entries whose series are both
  the last thirty days and one whose series is the same thirty days as a rate are
  three pictures that look identical and mean three things.
- Name ids in `emphasis`, not positions. A border that follows a position moves to a
  different entry after a reorder.
- Keep the figure in the units your `unit` names. A column of bare numerals reads
  badly, and a locale-correct grouping is a decision about your readers rather than
  one this Block can make.

### Content guidelines

- `name` is what a reader would say in conversation.
- `valueLabel` is a sentence about the figure, in your product's voice, and it is
  the reading every assistive technology is given.
- `seriesLabel` names the series, such as "runs per day over the last fortnight".
- `hrefLabel` says what following the link does. A link whose only words are a name
  tells a reader nothing.
