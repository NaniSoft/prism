## Overview

CaseStudy01 is one case study in full: a named customer, a sector, the problem,
what was done, a row of figures, and optionally a quotation, stacked or split.

The results row is the reason this Item exists, and every figure in it is the
caller's. A case study is the one page on a marketing site where a number is
legally dangerous. Every other number on a marketing page is decoration a reader
can discount, and a number under the word "results" is a statement of fact about
a third party that a publisher is accountable for.

So `results` is a prop, every field of it is a prop, `Metric` is handed the value
the consumer formatted, and there is not one figure, one percentage and one period
in this package. A Block that shipped a plausible reduction figure would be a false
advertising claim that installs itself into every consumer's product, on a page
nobody in this repository has any standing to publish.

`quoteLabel` is required whenever a quotation is given. It names the `figure` the
quotation sits in, so a reader is told it is a quotation rather than body copy.

## Usage

```tsx
import { CaseStudy01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/case-study-01'

<CaseStudy01
  customer="Northbank Clearing"
  sector="Clearing"
  summary="Nightly reconciliation moved from a spreadsheet to a run that reports its own failures."
  problem={<><h3>The problem</h3><p>Three teams reconciled by hand.</p></>}
  results={[
    { label: 'Reconciliation time', value: '4 min', delta: -0.86, hint: 'Median over 30 days' },
    { label: 'Unmatched lines', value: '11' },
  ]}
  quote={{ quote: 'We found out on a Monday that it had stopped.', name: 'A. Mensah', role: 'Operations' }}
  quoteLabel="In their words"
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use CaseStudy01 for one customer, on its own page, where the reader is deciding
whether a claim about somebody else is true. It is not a list: `case-studies-01`
is where an index of them belongs and this is the page one of them opens onto.

Use `layout="split"` when the reader came for the figures, and the default
stacked arrangement when the account is the point and the numbers support it.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a testimonial with no account behind it. A quotation on its own
is `testimonial-01`, which needs no figures and therefore makes no factual claim.

Do not use it for a company profile or an about page. Those have no results and
no third party in them, which is `about-01` and `story-01`.

Do not use it to publish a metric about your own product's behaviour. That is
`stats-01`, where the figures are about the product rather than about a customer.

### Best practices

- Never let a figure into this Block that the customer has not agreed to in
  writing. It is the one place in this package where an unused prop is a legal
  question rather than a style one.
- Format `value` yourself, in the unit it is in. `delta` is a bare number and
  `Metric` prints it exactly as passed, so `0.12` reads `0.12` and that is
  honest.
- Put the period and the source in `hint`, not in the label. The label is scanned
  and the caveat is read.
- Pass `quoteLabel` with any quotation, and make it say what the quotation is.
  A quotation a screen reader cannot tell from the page's own words is a
  misattribution waiting to happen.

### Content guidelines

- Write the summary as the account, not as the claim. The figures are the claim and
  they are below it.
- Name the customer as they name themselves, and publish nothing about them you
  would not repeat in a room with them in it.
- Attribute the quotation to a person who agreed to it, with the role they
  publish, and never paraphrase a sentence into a quotation.
