## Overview

Compliance01 is a table of what is claimed, to which standard, over what scope,
when it was last reviewed, and where the evidence is.

The reason this Item exists is not the table. A table is `Table`, and `Table` is
already in this package. What a design system is tempted to do on a compliance
surface is render a tick for a claim nobody has checked. The tick is the reward,
it is the thing the marketing copy reaches for, and a compliance section that
defaults its state to the good one is how a package ships a certification mark it
did not earn.

So the state set makes the honest answers first class: `in-progress` and
`not-applicable` are members rather than edge cases, which is what stops a
publisher from reading the two quiet states as an admission to be hidden by
claiming the first one instead. A row that claims to hold something with no
standard behind it throws rather than drawing a bare tick.

Every word in every cell is the caller's legal statement. That is load-bearing
here and not a formality: a compliance surface is a set of representations made to
regulators, customers and auditors, and a design system that contributed a word to
one would be publishing a representation it has no standing to make.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Compliance01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/compliance-01'

<Compliance01
  title="What we publish"
  columns={{
    claim: 'Claim',
    standard: 'Standard',
    scope: 'Scope',
    reviewedAt: 'Last reviewed',
    state: 'State',
    evidence: 'Evidence',
  }}
  rows={[
    {
      id: 'sso',
      claim: 'Single sign-on for every administrative surface',
      standard: 'OIDC',
      scope: 'Production estate',
      reviewedAt: '2026-06',
      state: 'held',
      stateLabel: 'Held',
      evidenceHref: 'https://example.com/docs/sso',
      evidenceLabel: 'Access control standard',
    },
  ]}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use Compliance01 where a page makes representations about standards, controls or
certifications and wants a reader to be able to check them. The evidence column
is the reason to reach for it: a claim with no published document behind it is a
marketing claim wearing a table.

Use it when the honest state of at least one row is not the good one. If every
row is `held` with a standard and a link, a list of certifications is the honest
thing and this Block is ceremony around it.

### When not to use

Do not use it as a marketing badge rail. A set of certification logos with no
claim, no scope, no review date and no evidence is a `logo-cloud-01`.

Do not use it for awards. A recognition has an awarding body and a year rather
than a standard and a review date, which is `awards-01`.

Do not use it to publish a status of your own service. That is
`status-ledger-01`, whose tiers are about what a product can do rather than what
a publisher attests to.

### Best practices

- Give every row a `standard` if it is in the `held` state. Without one the Block
  throws, and that is the point.
- Give every row words for its state. The tone is Prism's and the vocabulary is
  yours, because your regime says "attested" where another's says "held".
- Name the evidence in words, not with the document title alone. The name is the
  link's accessible name and a link announced by its address is punctuation.
- Put a relative reading beside the table when recency matters, by composing
  `relative-time` yourself. This Block prints the string you pass and formats
  nothing, because formatting a review date is a Component's job.

### Content guidelines

- Write each claim as the assertion a reader can check. "Encryption at rest" is
  checkable; "enterprise grade" is not, and the table will render it beside a
  standard as though it were.
- Scope every claim. A claim with no scope reads as a claim about everything,
  which is stronger than most publishers can support.
- Publish the weak states. A section that lists only what is held tells a reader
  that the publisher has nothing else, which is a larger claim than the table.
