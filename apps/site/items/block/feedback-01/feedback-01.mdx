## Overview

Feedback01 is the panel that asks a reader one question properly: a scale, a place to
say more, and optionally the thing they are talking about. The scale, the comment and
the attachment are each optional except the scale, and every string is passed in as a
prop.

**The scale is the caller's data, and this is the design decision.** Prism does not
supply five faces, three faces or any faces. The number of points and the words at
each end are product knowledge, and they differ per product:

| Product | Points | The ends |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Support | 3 | Resolved, Blocked |
| Product feedback | 5 | Not what I needed, Exactly what I needed |
| A rating scale | 10 | numbers the reader already knows |
| A design review | not ordered at all | and a radio group is the wrong control, which is the caller's call to make |

A five-point scale labelled from "bad" to "great" is a survey instrument, and a
feedback form usually is not one. It is one reader telling one team something they
could not have guessed, so the words at the ends are the whole instrument and Prism
has no standing to pick them.

## The scale is a radio group, and not five buttons

That is a decision with an accessibility consequence, not a visual one. A set of
mutually exclusive options is a radio group, and a screen reader announces the size
of the set and the checked state only when it is one. The keyboard model follows: the
arrows move inside the group and Tab crosses it as one stop.

Five buttons get the look right and everything else wrong. Each is a Tab stop, none
of them is a set, and a reader of them is told he is on the third of five
undifferentiated controls rather than that he has chosen three out of five.

## Usage

```tsx
import { useState } from 'react'
import { Feedback01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/feedback-01'

const SCALE = [
  { id: 'blocked', label: 'Blocked', value: 1 },
  { id: 'slow', label: 'Slow', value: 2 },
  { id: 'fine', label: 'Fine', value: 3 },
]

export function ReportAProblem() {
  const [rating, setRating] = useState<string>()
  const [comment, setComment] = useState('')

  return (
    <Feedback01
      eyebrow="Nexus"
      title="Tell us what went wrong"
      description="A support engineer reads every one of these."
      scale={SCALE}
      scaleLabel="How is it going?"
      value={rating}
      onValueChange={setRating}
      comment={{
        label: 'What happened?',
        value: comment,
        onValueChange: setComment,
        maxLength: 500,
        counter: true,
      }}
      submitLabel="Send"
      status={{ state: 'idle', message: null }}
      onSubmit={async (value) => {
        await openTicket({
          rating: value.rating,
          impact: value.ratingValue,
          note: value.comment,
          attachments: value.files,
        })
      }}
    />
  )
}
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where one reader has something to say to your team and your team will read it.
That is a support request, a product report, a bug with a screenshot attached.

Use the comment field whenever the scale alone cannot carry the message. A rating of
"blocked" with no note tells a team nothing they can act on.

Use the artefact field when the reader is describing something they can see. A
screenshot is worth more than a paragraph, and the panel renders the chosen files
with their sizes so a reader can see they picked the right one.

### When not to use

Do not use it as a survey. A survey has a sample, a scale chosen for comparability
across runs, and an instrument somebody validated. This panel is one reader telling
one team one thing, and the scale is theirs to change between releases.

Do not use it for a rating you will aggregate into a public score. That is a survey
instrument with the sampling problems of one, and a number nobody can trust is worse
than no number.

Do not use it when the reader has already told you. A confirmation panel asking
"was that helpful" immediately after a support reply is a second interruption in the
same conversation, and the reply itself is the better place.

Do not use it for a permission boundary. A reader who cannot see the thing cannot
report it, and a panel that accepts a report about a screen they never saw is
collecting noise.

### Best practices

- Write both ends of the scale as the reader's situation rather than as a judgement.
  "Blocked" names a fact about their day; "bad" names a judgement about your
  product, which is the judgement you are asking them for.
- Keep the scale short enough to read in one pass. More than seven points and a
  reader starts treating the middle of the scale as the safe answer.
- Read `ratingValue` rather than `rating` when you aggregate. `rating` is your own
  key and survives a reorder of the scale; `ratingValue` is the number, which is what
  a mean needs.
- Validate in your own handler. Prism has no minimum length, no required point and no
  file size rule, because each of those is a decision about your product and your
  data.
- Never wrap this Block so that `onSubmit` becomes optional. A feedback panel that
  collects a report and drops it is worse than no panel, and the required handler is
  what stops that quietly.

### Content guidelines

- Write the heading as the thing the reader is about to do, not as the feature they
  are reporting on. "Tell us what went wrong" is an action; "Feedback" is a
  category.
- Write the comment label as the question the note answers. "What happened?" tells
  a reader what to write; "Additional comments" tells them there is a box.
- Write the attachment label as what may be attached and nothing more. "A screenshot
  of the error" is a fact; "Attachments (optional, max 10 MB)" is a rule, and rules
  belong in your own hint line under the control where you can add them.
- Do not thank the reader in the heading. They have not sent anything yet, and the
  sentence that thanks them belongs in your own `status`, after you have it.
