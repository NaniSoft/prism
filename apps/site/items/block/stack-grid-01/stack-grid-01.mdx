## Overview

StackGrid01 is a survey of what a product is assembled from, and what it built
itself. The composed parts sit in one grid, each a name and a role; the in-house
work sits in a second grid, each a name and a line about it.

Three of the four NaniSoft sites publish this section, and each one drew it by
hand with the same two groups. Three of them also drew the second group
differently from the first - a dashed border, a different tile, a mark above the
name - because the visual difference is the claim: *this part is ours*.

That is what the `own` group is here. It is a separate prop and a separate grid
rather than a flag on the part, because the difference is a claim about provenance
and a claim is not a property of a row: a part that is assembled and a part that is
built are the same kind of thing with a different answer to "who wrote it".

## Usage

```tsx
import { StackGrid01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/stack-grid-01'

<StackGrid01
  title="How it is built"
  parts={[
    { name: 'Postgres', role: 'Storage' },
    { name: 'NATS', role: 'Queue' },
  ]}
  own={[{ name: 'The orchestrator', blurb: 'Decides what runs next.' }]}
  ownLabel="built in-house"
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use StackGrid01 where a reader's question is "what is this made of" and the honest
answer is mostly other people's work with a few exceptions.

Use it when the exceptions are the point. If everything in the grid is composed,
drop `own` and use `NoteGrid01` instead: a second group with nothing in it is a
claim you have not made.

### When not to use

Do not use it for integrations the reader connects themselves. That is a
`ProductGrid01` of destinations, and a survey of parts is not a survey of things
you can turn on.

Do not use it where you cannot substantiate the in-house group. The block requires
`ownLabel` precisely because the group is a claim, and a claim nobody can check
is marketing.

### Best practices

- Pass the words you mean for the claim. Three of the four sites write three
  different ones - "built in-house", "built by Nanisoft", "our own" - and the
  block does not choose between them.
- Keep a role to a phrase. The grid is a survey of what a product stands on, not a
  feature list, and a role that runs to a sentence turns a survey into prose.
- Order the composed parts by how much of the product they carry.

### Content guidelines

- Use each part's own name, as its authors spell it.
- Write a role as what the part does *here*, not as what the part is generally
  capable of.
- Write a blurb as why it is not assembled. That is the question the group raises.
