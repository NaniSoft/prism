## Overview

ProjectDashboard01 is one project's overview: a row of figures and a grid of titled
panels whose spans the caller declares. Every panel is a slot, and that is the
decision the whole Block rests on.

What a project overview leads with is a fact about the project. A pipeline wants
the run queue wide and the approvals narrow. An estate wants capacity wide and the
fleet list narrow. A research group wants the papers wide and the citations narrow.
A Block that chose one of those would be a template wearing a component's name, and
a consumer whose most important panel is the narrow one would have to fork the
Block to get it. So this Block owns three things and nothing else: the grid, the
spans, and the figures row.

The figures are `Metric` and the state is `Status`, and a panel's title is a heading
one step below the section, derived rather than written.

## Usage

```tsx
import { ProjectDashboard01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/project-dashboard-01'

<ProjectDashboard01
  eyebrow="Warehouse migration, phase two"
  name="What is left"
  summary="Everything on this page is a fact about this project, and the order of the panels is yours."
  state="blocked"
  stateLabel="Blocked on a credential"
  figures={[
    { label: 'Tables moved', value: '31 of 48' },
    { label: 'Rows rewritten', value: '418,204' },
  ]}
  panels={[
    { id: 'queue', title: 'Run queue', span: 'half', children: <YourRunQueue /> },
    { id: 'approvals', title: 'Approvals', span: 'half', children: <YourApprovals /> },
    { id: 'chart', title: 'Rows per day', span: 'full', toolbar: <YourRange />, children: <YourChart /> },
  ]}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it for one project, on a page that is trying to tell a reader everything at
once. Six panels is the point where a reader starts scrolling rather than reading,
so a seventh wants its own page or its own tab.

Use `span="full"` for the panel that is the point of the page, and `third` for the
panels a reader checks after it. The default is `half`, which works for any number
of panels: one is half a row, two are a row, three are a row and a half.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a project with nothing on it yet. An empty grid of empty cards is
a page of six frames, and a reader who sees it concludes the product is broken.

Do not use it to lay out something that is not a set of panels. A hero with a figure
is a `Hero01` and a rail with a position is a `ProcessRail01`.

Do not reach past the spans. A panel that wants a fifth of a row has no seam here,
and the answer is two panels side by side, which is also what reads better on a
phone.

### Best practices

- Pass `state` and `stateLabel` together, or neither. The run refuses exactly one of
  the two rather than drawing a dot with no sentence.
- Order the panels the way a reader should meet them. This Block does not reorder
  them to put a chart first, because a dashboard whose top panel is the one the
  product cares about is a claim the caller is better placed to make.
- Give every panel a title unless it is a figure with nothing to say above it. A
  panel with no title draws no heading, which is right for a chart and wrong for a
  list a reader has to recognise.
- Put a panel's own controls in its `toolbar`, not in a panel above it. A control
  the reader has to find in another panel is a control the reader does not use.
- Compose a bounded list with `ListPanel` inside the slot. A Block that drew the
  bounds would be deciding how long your list is allowed to be, which is a fact
  about your data.

### Content guidelines

- `name` is the project's own name, and it is the section heading.
- `summary` is one or two sentences about this project, not about the page.
- A figure's `value` is the figure. If the change needs a sentence, put the whole
  reading in `value` as your own node, because a delta here prints as a bare number.
- A panel's `title` is what a reader would call that panel, in your words.
