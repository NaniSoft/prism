## Overview

Calendar01 is a month of scheduled work: the published `mini-calendar` for the
month, the locale, the weekday headings and the paging, and your items grouped
under the days they fall on.

It is named `Calendar01` and not `Calendar01` because `calendar.tsx` is
already a Component in this package, and a Block with the same short name would be
two surfaces a reader could not tell apart in an import.

The grid is `mini-calendar` and the Block composes it rather than drawing a second
one, and that is a decision about keyboard behaviour rather than about dates. That
Component owns a roving tab stop so the grid is one stop rather than forty-two,
arrow keys that cross a month boundary, a `Home` and an `End` that go to the ends
of the row, a month name announced when the reader pages, a weekday row formatted
from the first week's own cells so a heading cannot drift from the column it sits
over, and a fixed six-week height so paging a month does not move the dates out
from under the pointer.

## The items are a day list, and that is a cost

The items are a list beside the grid rather than marks inside its cells, because
`mini-calendar` draws days and takes no content per day. Adding a slot to a
published Component for the sake of one Block would be a change to a surface four
products already compose, made in a Block's name, which is the arrangement this
repository keeps separate on purpose.

It is also the better reading for a queue: the grid answers which days have work on
them and the list answers what is on Tuesday, and a reader working a backlog wants
the second answer more often than the first. The cost is named rather than hidden.
A reader who wants to know that the fifteenth has anything on it has to look at
the list, and a consumer who needs the mark composes their own `mini-calendar`
beside this Block and passes the same `items` to both.

## The cap, and why it is never silent

A month grid with nine items on one day is a month grid nobody can read. Six pixel
entries in a forty pixel cell are not a denser calendar, they are a calendar a
reader has given up on, and the reader who gives up is the one who then believes
the day was quiet. So `maxPerDay` caps at three and the rest are named, in your own
words, by a required `overflowLabel`. A cap that hides items with no overflow
control is a silent loss, and it is the one failure a calendar may not have: it
would look finished when it is not.

## Moving an item is the caller's

A date view has one interaction a month grid does not: a move. Rescheduling an
item onto another day is a drag with a drop target, a new order, a write and a
conflict, and every one of the four is yours, so the Block exposes a per-item
`handle` node rather than a move callback. You fill it with your own control and
wire it to whatever owns your schedule, exactly as `Kanban01` leaves a board's
drag to a control you write into a card. The Block places the node and nothing
more: it draws no drag, reports no move and holds no order, because a drag is
behaviour a Block does not hold and a move is a write to a store this package
never sees.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Calendar01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/calendar-01'

<Calendar01
  eyebrow="Nexus"
  title="October"
  month={month}
  onMonthChange={setMonth}
  locale="en-GB"
  weekStartsOn={1}
  previousLabel="The month before"
  nextLabel="The month after"
  label="Scheduled work, by month"
  items={[
    { id: 'a', date: '2026-10-02', label: 'Access review with the data owner' },
    {
      id: 'c',
      date: '2026-10-05',
      label: 'Reconcile September invoices',
      state: 'warning',
      stateLabel: 'Blocked on billing',
    },
  ]}
  maxPerDay={3}
  overflowLabel={(count) => `${count} more on this day`}
  empty="Nothing is scheduled this month."
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it when the month is the unit a reader arrives with, and when the work is
placed on named days rather than on instants.

Use it beside a list when a reader's question is "what is on Tuesday" more often
than "what is this month".

### When not to use

Do not use it for events with a clock time. A grid of days cannot show an hour, and
a day with three timed things on it wants a `ListPanel` or a `DataTable01` sorted
by time.

Do not use it to pick a single date in a form. That is `DatePicker`, which owns
the field, the popover and the roving tab stop for a form control.

Do not use it for a release history. A `Changelog01` reads top to bottom, which is
the opposite of what a month grid is for.

### Best practices

- Pass `month` with `onMonthChange`, or pass `defaultMonth` and nothing. A
  controlled month with no `onMonthChange` draws two paging controls that cannot
  page.
- Pass `locale`. Nothing in the Block holds a month name, a weekday name or a digit
  set, so the locale tag is the whole of the translation surface.
- Pass `stateLabel` with every `state`. The five values are the `Status` tones
  themselves, so there is nothing to translate between two lists; the words are the
  only thing missing and the run fails without them.
- Write `date` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` key when you mean a calendar day. It is read as
  that day in the reader's own timezone, where a plain parse of the same string
  would put the item on the previous day for every reader west of Greenwich.
- Pass `empty` only when a quiet month would be mistaken for a broken one. The
  empty list beside the grid already says so.
- Raise `maxPerDay` for a month of single-item days. The overflow line stops
  appearing on its own.
- Attach your own drag to an item's `handle` rather than asking for a move
  callback. The Block holds no order and no drop target, so the move is your own
  write to your own array.

### Content guidelines

- `label` is the grid's accessible name and it says which question the grid
  answers. "Calendar" answers no question, and a page with two of them gives a
  reader two identical names.
- `previousLabel` and `nextLabel` are announced names for two controls with icons
  on them, so they have to be words. "Previous" in your language is enough.
- `label` on an item is what a reader would call the thing. A grid cell is not
  enough room for a sentence, so keep it to a few words and put the rest in the
  destination.
- `stateLabel` says what the tone is for. "Blocked on billing" is useful; "Warning"
  is the dot again.
- `overflowLabel` takes the number of hidden items and returns the whole sentence,
  because the sentence around a number belongs to the product.
