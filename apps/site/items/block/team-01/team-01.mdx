## Overview

Team01 is a grid or a list of people: a portrait where there is one, a name, a
role, a biography and a destination where the consumer publishes one. All four
NaniSoft sites publish a team section, and each one wrote it by hand, for the
people who watch a pipeline, who read a market, and who keep an estate observed.

The reason the section is worth a Block is the opposite of the usual one. It is
where a marketing page is most tempted to make a claim about a person: a card
with a portrait and a job title under it reads as an endorsement. So the
arrangement keeps the person's own name as the anchor, keeps the role in the
words the consumer wrote, and invents neither.

The biography is not truncated. A Block that cut a biography at three lines with
an ellipsis would be deciding which part of a person's account of their own work
matters, and cutting mid-sentence removes the qualification rather than the
flattery. A caller who wants three lines writes three lines.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Team01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/team-01'

<Team01
  title="Who works on this"
  variant="cards"
  highlight={['p-mehta']}
  people={[
    {
      id: 'p-mehta',
      name: 'Priya Mehta',
      role: 'Pipeline operations',
      bio: 'Watches every scheduled capture and reads the ones that stopped early.',
      avatar: { name: 'Priya Mehta' },
      href: 'https://example.com/people/priya-mehta',
      hrefLabel: 'Profile',
    },
  ]}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use Team01 on a page whose subject is people rather than a product: a team page, an
about page, a practice page that introduces the people who do the work. Between
one and twelve people reads well in the cards arrangement; past twelve a list or
an index serves a reader better than a grid.

Use `variant="list"` when the biographies run longer than a caption, and the
default cards arrangement when they are one sentence.

### When not to use

Do not use it to summarise a large group. `AvatarGroup` is the Component for a set
a page is pointing at rather than showing: a row of overlapping discs with a count
for the rest says the set is longer than the space, which is a statement about a
membership rather than about a person.

Do not use it for a testimonial or a quote from a person. A named quotation with
its context is `testimonial-01`.

Do not use it for members of a project with a presence and a last-seen column.
That is an application surface and it is `member-list-01`.

### Best practices

- Pass a `src` on the portrait, or pass the `avatar` field with a `name` and no
  `src` for the initials. Pass neither for a person the consumer has no
  photograph of, rather than passing a silhouette.
- Give `hrefLabel` whenever you pass `href`. A link announced by its address is
  punctuation a screen reader cannot act on, so the words naming the destination
  are required rather than optional.
- Pass `highlight` as ids, never as positions. A border is a claim about who
  matters on the page, and a claim that follows a sort is a claim that drifts.
- Leave the biography at whatever length it is. If a card is too tall, write a
  shorter biography.

### Content guidelines

- Write roles as the people write them. "Platform lead" and "Head of Platform"
  are different claims about a person and the second one is often not true.
- Do not put an endorsement in a role line. The arrangement around a portrait
  already reads as one.
- Keep the register even across the set. One biography in the first person and
  five in the third reads as a recruitment page inside a company page.
