## Overview

Account01 is an account in full: a name, a kind, a reference, a set of facts, a
balance or a plan reading, a slot for the caller's own recent activity, the regions
and surfaces the account spans, and a slot for the caller's own controls.

The storefront version of this pattern is a customer record, and the shape is
familiar. A shop shows somebody their customer record: who the account is, what is
on it, what it owes, what happened on it recently, and the controls the account's
owner is entitled to press. What changes here is whose account it is and who reads
it. A NaniSoft account is a workspace rather than a person, and the reader is very
often not the person who created it.

## This is not a settings page

An account is a fact about who owes what and what they may do. A settings page is a
set of controls that change one. They are read in opposite directions, they are
built by different teams, and conflating them is how a read surface turns into a
screen where a mistake is a data change.

So this Block ships no control of its own. No change plan button, no close button,
no update card, no edit reference. `controls` and `activity` are slots, and each
control inside them is your own Component with your own permission check, your own
confirmation and your own sentence. A Block that drew a "Close account" button would
be shipping a write path into a read surface, and the harm of that is not a styling
error: a record is read by people who did not create it, so a control on it is a
control a reader may press about somebody else's money, and the person who finds the
bug is neither the person who wrote the code nor the person who pressed the button.

If you want one screen with the record above and the settings below, compose
`Account01` inside your own `Section` and put `SettingsPanel01` under it. The
outline then has a heading for each, which is the honest arrangement for two
different jobs.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Account01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/account-01'

<Account01
  eyebrow="Nexus"
  name="Northgate Estates"
  kind="customer"
  kindLabel="Paying customer on annual terms"
  reference="NX-CUS-004471"
  facts={[
    { id: 'created', label: 'Customer since', value: <RelativeTime date={record.createdAt} /> },
    { id: 'region', label: 'Billing region', value: 'United Kingdom' },
    { id: 'owner', label: 'Account owner', value: 'Bo Lindqvist' },
  ]}
  balance={{ label: 'Owes on the current invoice', value: { amount: 1204.6, currency: 'GBP' } }}
  plan={{ name: 'Pipeline, annual', href: '/plans/pipeline', hrefLabel: 'Read the plan' }}
  states={[
    { id: 'capture', name: 'Capture estate', state: 'degraded', stateLabel: 'Running with two rejected batches' },
    { id: 'pipeline', name: 'Pipeline', state: 'active', stateLabel: 'Running' },
  ]}
  activity={<EventFeed events={events} />}
  controls={
    <Button variant="outline">Open the account</Button>
  }
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where a reader came to find out about one account and not to change it. A
support engineer checking a workspace, somebody in finance reconciling a line, an
administrator reading a record they did not create.

Use it where the reader's questions are "who owes what" and "what state is it in".
Both are answered by scanning, which is why the regions are a bounded list rather
than a table.

### When not to use

Do not use it for the account's own settings. That is `settings-panel-01`, and a
record with controls on it is a page where a reader is not sure whether looking is
the same as changing.

Do not use it for a person. A colleague with a portrait, a role and a biography is
`user-profile-01`, and an account is not a person even when one person is on it.

Do not use it for a single figure. A balance on its own is a `Metric`, and a
balance with a plan and a renewal date and nothing else is a `FactList` with three
rows.

### Best practices

- Treat `controls` and `activity` as slots for your own Components, and put your
  permission check inside each control rather than around the Block. The Block
  cannot know who is reading.
- Pass `reference` and expect it to render in the mono face. That is deliberate: an
  identifier is machine notation, the same rule `SectionHeading` states for its own
  `index` prop, and a contract reference is the one string on the page a reader may
  read aloud.
- Map your own state words into `stateLabel` and expect the tone table to fall back
  to the supporting ink for anything it does not hold. The value is on the row as
  `data-state` so you can see which ones need mapping.
- Use `layout="split"` on a wide screen where the reader is comparing two accounts
  in one session. It moves no control, only the column they sit in.
- Let the fact list be empty. An account with a name, a kind and a balance is a
  complete record, and the Block draws no box where a fourth fact would have been.

### Content guidelines

- `name` is the account's own name as your product writes it. It is the section
  heading, so a reader who arrives by link can navigate back to it.
- `kind` is what the account is to your product: a customer, a subscriber, a
  partner, a trial. It is the supporting line under the name, not a fact, because
  it is the answer to the question the reader arrived with.
- `stateLabel` is a sentence your readers would say. It is required, and the machine
  value is never printed in its place.
- `balance.note` and `plan.detail` are where the qualifying sentence goes: the
  period, the credit note, the renewal date, the link to the invoice.
- `defaultLabel` on a billing source and `stateLabel` here are the same rule in two
  places: the words are the information and the colour is the reminder, and a mark
  with no words beside it is a claim no reader can check.
