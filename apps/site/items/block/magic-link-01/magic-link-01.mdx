## Overview

MagicLink01 is a passwordless sign-in: one address field, one control that posts a
link to it, the caller's own sentence about what is about to arrive, and a confirmation
that keeps the sent address on screen in full beside a control to change it. Every
string is passed in as a prop, and the Block ships no sentence of its own anywhere on
the page.

The storefront's version of this pattern is the magic-link card: an email field, a
button, and a line that says a link is on its way, whose purpose is to let a shopper
past the gate without inventing a password they would then have to remember. The
composition here is that card, unchanged. What the reader is trusting is a link that
expires and cannot be replayed by whoever finds the inbox, which is why the two states
below are kept apart.

## Two states, and keeping them apart is the whole design

Not yet sent, and sent. The obvious arrangement is a screen that replaces its form with
a confirmation the instant the link goes out, which is what the storefront version
does, and it is the arrangement that makes this screen dangerous on an instrument.

A link posted to a mistyped address produces a page that says it worked. The reader
opens an inbox that will never contain anything, decides the product is broken, and
tries again with a second address, which is now two accounts and a support ticket. A
screen that keeps the address on the screen, in full, beside a control to change it,
turns four minutes into one glance.

So `sent` is a prop rather than an internal state: you say the link is out, the Block
draws the address and the change control and nothing else. The cost is named rather
than hidden, which is that a reader who arrives at a confirmation with no `sent` on
screen sees a form again and will press it again, so you have to pass `sent` at the same
moment you pass a `status` of `sent`. That is one piece of state in step with another
in your own reducer, and it is stated here because the alternative was a Block that
invented its own sent flag and then could not know whether the link it believed it had
sent was the one your server had.

## The resend control is a separate action from submit

The two look identical from the outside and are not the same at all. Submitting posts a
link for an address the reader has just typed. Asking again posts a link for an address
that is already on the screen in the confirmation. A reader who opened the wrong inbox
needs the second, and a screen that offers one control for both makes that reader
retype an address they have already confirmed in order to get the thing they were
already offered.

So the confirmation carries its own control, your `onResend` receives the address the
link was sent to rather than whatever happens to be in a field, and the control is
named twice. Both names are required, because a control that reads the same sentence
before and after the reader pressed it is an invitation to press it again against a
limit the Block cannot see. The Block will also refuse to draw the control at all
unless you pass `sent`, so a caller who passes a handler and two labels without a
confirmation is told so rather than left with a function that composes and discards.

## Why the description is required here and optional everywhere else

Press the control and nothing visible happens. No page loads, no code appears on the
screen, no field changes. A reader who does not know a link is about to be posted
treats the form as broken, presses it again, and the reader is right about the form and
wrong about the product. A screen that can be pressed three times in four seconds meets
a rate limiter before the reader has read the sentence that was there all along.

So the type makes it required rather than the Block defaulting to something. The cost
is named: a product that genuinely has nothing to say about what arrives has to write
the sentence anyway, and it is usually the sentence it should have written on its
sign-in screen too.

There is no in-flight state in this Block's `status` union, so the submit control is
never disabled and a reader can press it as many times as they like while your queue
works. That is stated rather than papered over: the caller's way to refuse a second
press is to pass `sent`, which takes the form off the screen, and the sentence in the
heading is what carries the wait.

## The three decisions this screen has to make, and why none of them is Prism's

| Decision | What a Block that made it would be |
| --- | --- |
| Whether the address exists | An account directory, and the enumeration oracle a sign-in surface refuses to be, on the one screen where a reader has just been told a passwordless sign-in is available. |
| How many links can be asked for | A rate limiter in a component library, shipping one policy to every product that installed it. |
| Whether the address was right | A format check guessed on the reader's behalf, on the one field where a wrong guess is a lockout for somebody whose address is unusual and perfectly deliverable. |

The honest failure is yours, and a mail transport has more outcomes than one sentence can
be true of: accepted and delivered, accepted and filtered, accepted and delayed, accepted
and bounced, refused at the first hop, and refused because your queue is full. A screen
that says "we sent it" is making a claim about a transport it has never spoken to. So
`status` is a prop, there is no `sent` flag and no `error` flag in the file, and the
outcome is drawn in one live region that is rendered on both sides of the state change
rather than one per state, because a live region that appears together with its first
message is the one case a screen reader is least reliable about.

## What the Block refuses

| Refused | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A blank `identifier.label` | The only name on the page would be "text field", which says nothing about what it is for. |
| A blank `submitLabel` | A reader would have to guess that pressing it posts a link to an inbox. |
| `identifier.value` with no `onValueChange` | A reader who mistyped an address is the reader who needs to clear it. |
| `onResend` with fewer than two names | One name means the control reads the same sentence before and after the reader pressed it. |
| `onResend` with no `sent` | The control has nowhere to be drawn, and the address it would resend to is not on the screen. |
| A `sent` with no `changeLabel` | The control that brings the form back is a button a reader can reach and not identify. |

## Usage

```tsx
'use client'

import { useState } from 'react'
import { MagicLink01, type MagicLink01Sent, type MagicLink01Status } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/magic-link-01'

export function PasswordlessSignIn() {
  const [address, setAddress] = useState('')
  const [status, setStatus] = useState<MagicLink01Status>()
  const [sent, setSent] = useState<MagicLink01Sent>()

  async function post(value: { identifier: string }, again: boolean) {
    setStatus({ state: 'working', message: again ? 'Posting another link.' : 'Posting the link.' })
    const result = await postMagicLink(value.identifier)
    if (result.undeliverable) {
      setStatus({ state: 'error', message: 'That domain cannot receive mail, so nothing was posted.' })
      return
    }
    if (again) {
      setStatus({ state: 'resent', message: 'Another link is on its way to the same address.' })
      return
    }
    setStatus({ state: 'sent', message: 'A link is on its way. It is good for fifteen minutes.' })
    setSent({
      identifier: value.identifier,
      expiresLabel: 'Good for fifteen minutes, and it stops working the moment it is used once.',
      changeLabel: 'Use a different address',
      onChange: (identifier) => {
        setAddress(identifier)
        setSent(undefined)
        setStatus(undefined)
      },
    })
  }

  return (
    <MagicLink01
      eyebrow="Nexus"
      title="Sign in without a secret phrase"
      description="We post a link to the address below and you open it on the device you want signed in. There is no phrase to remember and nothing to type into a second field."
      identifier={{ label: 'Work email', autoComplete: 'email', value: address, onValueChange: setAddress }}
      submitLabel="Post the link"
      onSubmit={(value) => post(value, false)}
      onResend={(identifier) => post({ identifier }, true)}
      resendLabel="Post another link to this address"
      resendConfirmLabel="Another link is on its way"
      status={status}
      sent={sent}
    />
  )
}
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where a product has decided that a phrase is the wrong second thing to ask for,
and has a mail transport that can be trusted to arrive. The address is the only field
and the type says so, because a link is posted.

Pass `sent` at the moment your server says the link is out, and pass a `status` of
`sent` in the same render. Those are two props in step, and they are the cost of not
letting the Block invent a flag.

Pass `expiresLabel` wherever your links expire, and make it a sentence. It is the one
line a reader uses to decide whether to go and look in an inbox now or later.

### When not to use

Do not use it where the address is not an inbox. A handle, a staff number and a
workspace name all identify a person here, and none of them receives a link.

Do not use it as a second factor. A reader who has already proved who they are and is
being asked for another proof is on `two-factor-01`, and a magic link as a second step
means the reader is waiting on a message for a factor that was supposed to be faster.

Do not use it twice on one page. Two identical forms compete and the second one reads
as broken.

Do not expect the Block to tell the reader the link was delivered. It cannot know, and
the sentence that claims it is yours to write and yours to be wrong about.

### Best practices

- Pass both resend names up front even if you expect only one of the two states. The
  run throws with fewer than two, and the state you will reach is the one you did not
  plan for.
- Report the enumeration question on purpose. "That address cannot receive mail" and
  "we sent a link anyway" leak different facts, and only you know which of them your
  threat model allows.
- Keep the expiry in the caller's words and in the caller's clock. A Block that
  formatted a duration would be choosing a locale and a granularity on a reader's
  behalf, on the one line where being vague reads as a shorter life than the link has.
- Pass `className` for grid placement only. A credential surface at the full container
  width is a form with a very long row of labels.

### Content guidelines

- Write the title as the absence of a phrase, not as a feature. "Sign in without a
  secret phrase" tells a reader what is not going to happen.
- Write the description as the mechanism, the destination and the wait. It is the only
  thing standing between the reader and a second press.
- Write the address label as the inbox to use, not as the field's name. "Work email"
  beats "Email address", and the label is also the field's accessible name.
- Write `changeLabel` as a sentence about what pressing it does. "Use a different
  address" is a control a voice control user can say out loud; "change" is a noun.
- Write `resendLabel` and `resendConfirmLabel` as two different actions. The first asks
  for a link and the second reports that one was asked for, and a reader who sees the
  same sentence twice presses twice.
- Write the `sent` message as a fact about the reader's next action. "A link is on its
  way" tells them what to do; "we sent you a link" is about you.
