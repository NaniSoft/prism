## Overview

Onboarding01 is a setup checklist: a set of steps, each with a state, an optional
action and an optional destination, a progress figure whose sentence is the
caller's, and a completion note the caller writes.

The two forms answer different questions, and `checklist` is the default because a
reader who has been here once is looking for the one thing left. A rail of numbered
steps is a document about the order, which is what a reader needs on a first run and
is one screen too much on a return visit. `steps` is for the first run, when the
order is the point, and it composes the rail.

A blocked step is a first-class state. A checklist that can only say done, and not
done and next, will read as broken to a reader who is genuinely stuck, and a reader
who thinks a product is broken stops trusting the product.

It is a client Component, because `onSelect` is a handler and a handler cannot be
passed across a server boundary.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Onboarding01, type OnboardingStep } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/onboarding-01'

<Onboarding01
  eyebrow="Getting started"
  title="Three things and you are running"
  steps={STEPS}
  progressLabel={(done, total) => `${done} of ${total} done`}
  completion="The last step can wait until Monday. Nothing runs until you do it."
  empty="This workspace has nothing to set up."
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use `checklist` for a reader who has been here before, which is most of the time.
The one outstanding thing should be findable in a glance, and the list of what is
outstanding is the shape that makes it findable.

Use `steps` for the first run, when the reader has never seen the setup and needs
to know what comes after what. The rail is refused anything a rail has no room for:
a description, a control, a destination, and a blocked step, because a blocked step
is a fact about this run rather than a position in the order.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a document about how a product works. That is prose, and `Prose`
in a section is the honest surface.

Do not use it for a wizard. A wizard owns its own position, its own validation and
its own back button, and a Block that owned those would be a wizard that four
products had to rewrite.

Do not set `onSelect` on a checklist whose steps carry their own actions. A row
cannot be a control and contain one, and the run says so.

### Best practices

- Pass `progressLabel` as a function of the two counts. It has a plural and every
  language words it differently, and a Block that composed it would compose the
  English one.
- Pass a `stateLabel` for every step, and make the four of them read as four
  different things to do rather than four shades of the same one.
- Mark a blocked step `blocked` rather than leaving it `current`. A step that has
  not moved in three days is not the step the reader is on, and drawing it as the
  current one is the arrangement that makes a reader think the product is broken.
- Put your own control in a step's `action` rather than composing around this Block.
  Which control a step offers is your fact: a connect, a retry, a skip.
- Write `completion` for the reader who has just finished the last step. That is the
  one moment a setup is read all the way through.

### Content guidelines

- `name` is what the step is, in the words a reader would use in conversation.
- `description` is for a reader who has arrived at the step, so it can be a
  sentence, a link or a code sample.
- `stateLabel` is your voice. "Connected" and "Waiting on your key" are the same
  position in a setup and two different things for a reader to do about it.
- `hrefLabel` says what following the link does.
