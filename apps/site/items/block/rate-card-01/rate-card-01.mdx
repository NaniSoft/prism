## Overview

RateCard01 is a price list per unit: what something costs, measured by a unit the
caller names, with the period, the allowance, the overage rate, a footnote and a
slot for the full document.

Every amount composes the `Price` Component rather than printing a currency. A
rate card is the one Block where a hardcoded symbol would be a legal claim about a
market, and it is still the wrong answer, because the symbol is the least of what a
price needs. A symbol says the currency and nothing else, while a rate card also
has a currency code, a precision, a period and a locale, and `Intl` already knows
all four.

The `tier` is the caller's word and there is no ladder in the type. A flat rate
with a floor, a per-market price and a per-seat price are all real rate cards and
none of them fits a volume ladder.

## Usage

```tsx
import { RateCard01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/rate-card-01'

<RateCard01
  title="What one agent run costs"
  unit="agent run"
  labels={{ tier: 'Band', included: 'Runs included', overage: 'Each run past that' }}
  rates={[
    { id: 'first', tier: 'First 500 runs', amount: { amount: 0, currency: 'USD' }, included: 'The whole allowance' },
    {
      id: 'next',
      tier: 'Next 20,000 runs',
      amount: { amount: 0.024, currency: 'USD' },
      overage: { amount: 0.031, currency: 'USD' },
    },
  ]}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use RateCard01 where a product charges by a unit the reader can count: a run, a
gigabyte, a node, a call. It is the block for metered pricing, where the number
that matters is small and the reader is checking it.

Use it where the pricing is a list of bands. A single flat price is one rate, and
one rate renders correctly: the header row, the row and the columns are the same
with one as with six, and nothing on this Block assumes a second.

### When not to use

Do not use it for plan tiers, which are sets of features rather than bands of
usage. That is `Pricing01` or `PricingCompare01`.

Do not use it for a single headline figure. A rate card is a list, and a list of
one is a sentence that wanted to be a paragraph.

### Best practices

- Name the unit precisely. The rate column is headed by it, so "usage" gives a
  reader nothing and "captured gigabyte per month" gives them the whole card.
- Pass the period on the amount, not on the unit. "/mo", "per month" and "a month"
  are three products' decisions and the cell has room for whichever one is yours.
- Use the `included` string rather than a number for an allowance. "Ten thousand
  rows", "the first five gigabytes" and "10000" are three different claims and only
  the first two are ones a reader can check.
- Let the allowance and overage columns follow the data. A card with no
  `overage` on any rate has no overage column, and you do not have to say so.
- Put the rounding rule and the billing granularity in `footnote`. That is the
  sentence that qualifies every number above it.

### Content guidelines

- Write a `tier` as a short noun phrase naming the band, not the price.
- Write the three column names in the product's own vocabulary. They are the only
  words on the card that are not data, and a reader who cannot name a column has
  to count from the left.
- Write `unit` in the singular if the amount is per one of something. The heading
  sits above a column of singular prices and a plural reads as a count of batches.
