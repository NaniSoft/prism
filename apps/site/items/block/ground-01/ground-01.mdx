## Overview

Ground01 is the page's one continuous ground: an aurora wash and a particle field
fixed behind every section, with the bar's transparency armed by the same Block. A
page that wears it reads as one surface rather than as a hero and a set of separate
bands, because the ground stays put while the sections scroll over it.

It exists because the ground was always made of published parts that every
application assembled by hand. The `site-atmosphere` class and the bar's
transparency are the stylesheet's; the wash, the field and the scroll state are
Components. Before this Block, one application wore the assembly on every route and
another on a single page, one dimmed the wash with a wrapper opacity and another ran
it at full presence, and no two of them disagreed on purpose. The Block is the
composition itself, so the decision is made once and a consumer makes it by choosing
props.

Every translucent surface involved names the same ladder. The wash's planes carry
`veil` steps, and the bar that turns to glass over the ground carries the
`veil-glass` step, so the figure below the headline and the surface above it are
priced by one scale. This is what keeps four translucent surfaces that mean the same
thing from wearing four numbers, which is the defect the veil scale was written to
retire.

## Usage

At layout scope, so every route of a marketing site wears one ground:

```tsx
import { Ground01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/ground-01'

export default function RootLayout({ children }) {
  return (
    <html lang="en">
      <body>
        <Ground01 />
        {children}
      </body>
    </html>
  )
}
```

On a single page, the ground arrives and leaves with it, and the page gives its own
`main` the `site-main` slot so the stacking contract holds: the ground sits at
`z-index: -1` over the body's background, and the content column is the stacking
context above it.

A console does not take this Block. A fixed field behind an instrument panel is a
distraction rather than a setting, so a working surface composes an `AuroraWash` on
its own `subtle` intensity inside the column its reader works in and keeps the rail
on a still ground.

## Guidelines

### When to use

- A marketing surface whose first screen carries a hero, and whose sections should
  read as one page rather than as stacked bands. Wear it at layout scope with
  `intensity="present"` and the transparent bar.
- A page whose reader works rather than reads: keep the fixed ground off it, and put
  a `subtle` wash behind the working column instead, so the light is a setting
  rather than a claim.

### When not to use

- Behind long-form reading. The docs pages of this site wear no ground, because a
  moving medium behind a passage a reader is trying to finish is a tax on the
  reading rather than a setting for it.
- As a per-section backdrop. A band whose content sits over a running figure is
  what `Backdrop01` is: an in-flow figure with a veil between it and the copy. This
  Block is the page-level ground, and the two compose rather than substitute.

### The contract the page holds up

The ground renders `aria-hidden` and takes no pointer, because a medium is not
information and a reader never reaches into a setting. A reader who asks the
platform for less motion gets the same ground at rest: the wash's planes stop on
their orbit, and the field stands on its server-rendered floor, which is complete
before any script runs.
