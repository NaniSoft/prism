## Overview

Consent01 is a cookie and tracking notice with two peers, a route to the caller's
own policy, and no memory. It is the only Block in the package where the styling is
the argument, because the failure it refuses is a styling decision that a design
system would otherwise ship once and four consumers would inherit.

The obvious way to build this panel is a filled accept beside a `ghost` reject. It
is what every marketing site already has, and a filled button is what a button is
for. It is also a dark pattern, and a design system shipping it is worse than a
product shipping it, because the design system shipped the styling and no consumer
chose it.

So the reject is drawn at `outline`: the same height, the same padding, the same
coarse-pointer target and a border a reader can see, beside an accept that is filled.
The two differ by fill and by nothing else, and the reader is not asked to weigh
them.

| Control | Weight | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept | `default`, filled | One of two decisions. |
| Reject | `outline`, the same size | The other decision, at the same reach. A `ghost` here is the defect this Block exists to prevent. |
| Settings | `ghost`, smaller | A route rather than a third decision, and the reader who wants neither is the reader who needs it. |
| Policy | `ghost` link | The one control a reader may never press. |

## Both handlers are required

`onAccept` and `onReject` are both required, so there is no arm of this component in
which a reader is shown a notice they cannot refuse. That is the same mechanism
`HeroAction` uses for the same reason: the shape that forbids something says so in
the type rather than in prose.

An optional `onReject` is how a one-way notice ships. A consumer who has not built a
refusal path yet leaves the prop off, the Block draws the accept and nothing else,
and the page is compliant with nobody.

## The Block persists nothing

There is no cookie, no `localStorage`, no `document.cookie` and no state, so a page
load shows the notice again until the caller stops rendering it. That is the correct
default for a notice about something still true.

A stored consent decision is a different thing and a deliberate act: a record in the
consumer's own storage that outlives the notice. Its correctness depends on the
consumer's jurisdictions, on how long their records are kept, on whether a reader who
clears their browsing data has to be asked again, and on whether a change to the
policy invalidates the last decision. Prism has no standing to decide any of those,
so the decision and its consequences are the consumer's.

## Usage

```tsx
import { useState } from 'react'
import { Consent01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/consent-01'

export function CookieNotice() {
  const [decision, setDecision] = useState<{ choice: 'accepted' | 'rejected'; at: string } | null>(
    null,
  )

  if (decision !== null) return null

  return (
    <Consent01
      title="Cookies on this site"
      body={
        <>
          <p>
            We set two cookies: one remembers that you have seen this notice, and one
            measures which pages are read so we know what to write next. The second is
            the only one that leaves your browser, and refusing it turns off exactly
            that.
          </p>
          <p>
            We do not sell anything to anyone and we do not share it with an ad
            network. The full list is in the policy.
          </p>
        </>
      }
      acceptLabel="Accept"
      rejectLabel="Refuse"
      settingsLabel="Choose which cookies"
      onAccept={() => record('accepted')}
      onReject={() => record('rejected')}
      onSettings={() => openPreferences()}
      policyHref="/legal/cookies"
      policyLabel="Read the cookie policy"
    />
  )
}
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where your own counsel and your own jurisdictions say a reader has to be told
before anything is stored on their device, and where the decision has to be
recorded.

Render it until the decision exists. A notice that disappears on its own has lied
about being asked, and a notice that never comes back is a notice the reader has
already answered somewhere you cannot see.

Use `position="banner"` by default. It is the arrangement a reader can scroll past
with the page still in front of them.

Pass `position="dialog"` only where a jurisdiction or a layout makes covering the
content the requirement. Then the cost is yours, and it is the cost named below.

### When not to use

Do not use it as a newsletter sign-up, a trial prompt or anything that is a
marketing message wearing a notice's clothes. Those are `Newsletter01` and
`Cta01`, and a reader who cannot tell them apart learns to dismiss the lot.

Do not use it on a page with no storage, no trackers and no processors. A notice
about nothing is the fastest way to teach a reader that notices are noise.

Do not compose a `dialog` arm and call the result a modal. This Block does not set
`role="dialog"`, does not make the page inert and does not trap focus, so a reader
can Tab out of the panel into content behind a scrim that says the page is
unavailable. If you need the behavioural half, use `Dialog` and put this Block's
`banner` inside it.

Do not pin it to the viewport with `className`. Prism pins nothing, because a
position in the reader's window is a position in your layout, and a Block that
claimed one would be a Block you had to move.

### Best practices

- Store the decision yourself and key it to the version of the policy it was taken
  against. A record with no policy version cannot answer the question a regulator
  asks about it, and it is a record in your storage, not a flag in this Block.
- Decide what a reader who clears their browsing data has to do, and write it down
  before you ship. Re-asking is annoying and not asking is unlawful in some
  places, and the Block has no opinion.
- Keep the body in the same voice as the rest of your product. It is read by a
  person deciding whether to trust you, and a paragraph of boilerplate under a
  plain heading is the paragraph that gets skimmed.
- Link to the policy, and make the label say what the reader gets. The link is
  required, so the only decision is what the words are.
- Compose `Button` yourself if you want a different balance between the two. Prism
  will not be restyled, and the reason the balance is decided here rather than in
  each product is that four products would each decide it differently.
- Re-ask when the policy changes. Nothing in this Block expires a record, because
  nothing in this Block holds one.

### Content guidelines

- Write the heading as the fact, not as the ask. "Cookies on this site" is a fact;
  "We value your privacy" is a claim a reader is being asked to accept without
  being told anything.
- Write the body as what is set and what refusing changes. Two short paragraphs beat
  a paragraph with a link in the middle, and a reader who cannot tell what refusing
  does is a reader who accepts.
- Name the processors, or say there are none. "We share nothing with anyone" is a
  sentence about your agreements and it is the sentence most worth writing.
- Write the three labels as three different actions. "Accept", "Refuse" and "Choose
  which cookies" tell a reader three things; "OK", "Decline" and "Manage" make them
  work it out.
- Write the policy label as what the reader gets rather than as the document's name.
