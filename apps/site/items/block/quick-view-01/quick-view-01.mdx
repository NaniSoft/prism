## Overview

QuickView01 is a summary panel a reader opens without leaving the page: a title, a short
body, a specification list, an optional mark, and up to two actions, presented in a
`Dialog` the caller controls.

The storefront version of this pattern is a quick-view modal on a product tile: the
item's name, one paragraph, three or four attributes, the price, and an add to basket
button. Every part of the layout transfers unchanged, and the parts are the same parts
a reader of a capability page meets. What does not transfer is the noun in the panel
and the transaction at the bottom. The panel here summarises a capability the product
offers, and the action at the bottom is a decision rather than a purchase.

## The rule this Block exists to enforce

**A quick view may not be the only place the information exists.** Everything a quick
view shows, a reader must be able to reach some other way: the card underneath, a row
in the table behind it, a section further down the page.

That is the same law `Carousel` states about everything a carousel shows, and a quick
view is a carousel's mistake with a dialog instead of a slider. It hides content behind
a deliberate act, it costs the reader a modal and a focus trap to see, and it takes the
content off the page when they close it.

A carousel whose images exist nowhere else is a set of images a reader on a keyboard,
on a screen reader, or with the controls hidden by a stylesheet cannot see at all. A
quick view whose specification exists nowhere else is a specification a reader has to
know exists before they can find it.

**The Block cannot enforce it.** There is no prop that would tell it the page also
carries the summary, and a Block that took one would take a boolean a caller could
pass and forget. So the law is in this documentation, where a caller meets it, and the
caller is the only party who can keep it.

## Why the overlay is `Dialog`

A panel built on a `div` and a `keydown` listener gets the Escape key and no focus
trap, so a keyboard reader tabs behind the panel into a page they cannot see, and the
defect is invisible in review because the panel works for the person writing it.

The focus trap, the restore of focus to whatever opened it, the Escape key, the outside
press, the scroll lock and the portal all come from `Dialog` and are all solved once.
What this Block adds is the three things `Dialog` does not know about: the summary's
own shape, the title at the caller's heading level, and the statement of the law above.

The cost is the one required prop, `closeLabel`. `Dialog` draws a dismiss control in
its corner, and that control is the one thing every reader needs a name for. It is a
prop rather than a default so a product whose interface never uses the word this
package's default would carry is a consumer who cannot fix it.

## It does not compose `Section`

Every other Block opens with `Section` so it inherits the container and the vertical
rhythm. A modal has no container to inherit: `Section` is the page column with the
page's vertical padding, and putting that inside a panel at most
`max-w-overlay-dialog` produces a page layout applied to a four-hundred pixel box.

`Lightbox` and `SearchDialog` are absent from `Section` for the same reason. The
corollary is that this Block draws no `SectionHeading` either, and its heading is its
own element, aligned flush left by construction rather than by a prop.

## Usage

```tsx
'use client'

import { useState } from 'react'

import { QuickView01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/quick-view-01'

export function Summaries() {
  const [openAt, setOpenAt] = useState<number | null>(null)
  const current = rows[openAt ?? 0]

  return (
    <>
      {/* the grid, which already carries everything the panel shows */}
      <QuickView01
        open={openAt !== null}
        onOpenChange={(open) => !open && setOpenAt(null)}
        closeLabel="Close the summary"
        headingLevel="h4"
        title={current.name}
        facts={current.facts}
      />
    </>
  )
}
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where a reader is choosing between capabilities on a page and wants to check one
without losing their place. That is a grid of cards, a table of rows, or a list of
short entries where the decision needs three more lines than the row has room for.

Use it with a controlled `open`, so the panel closes when the thing it describes has
gone: a row deleted, a filter that no longer includes it, a route that moved.

### When not to use

Do not use it for content that exists nowhere else. That is the law above, and a panel
built that way is a page a reader cannot link to, cannot bookmark, and cannot return to
with the back button.

Do not use it for a decision or a short task. That is what `Dialog` is for, and a
Dialog with a form in it is a better answer than a summary with a form in it.

Do not use it for three actions. Three actions is where a summary stops being a
summary and becomes a page, and the Block will not stop you, because a slot cannot be
counted.

### Best practices

- Keep the panel to the lines a reader checks before they decide to leave. About six is
  the honest ceiling; past that the panel is a page.
- Pass `headingLevel` as the level the thing you open it from sits at. A dialog's title
  is still a heading in the outline of the page that opened it, so a panel opened from
  a card under an `h3` wants an `h4`.
- Pass `size` rather than utilities. `sm` is two or three lines, `md` is the default,
  and `lg` is for a summary carrying more than four lines.
- Draw the trigger yourself. The trigger is a claim about where the control goes, and
  the four answers, a card's whole tile, a button beside a name, a row, a keypress, are
  each right somewhere.
- Close on navigation. Follow a link out of the panel and the panel should not be
  waiting behind the page you land on.

### Content guidelines

- Write the title as the thing's own name. It is the panel's accessible name and the
  only heading in it.
- Write the body as one or two sentences a reader can act on, not as the page's
  introduction. The page has one.
- Write `closeLabel` as your own interface's word for closing something. It is read out
  by a screen reader and seen by nobody, because `Dialog` draws the control as an icon.
- Write the `mark` so it works on a tinted square, or pass nothing. A panel that
  reserves room for a mark that is not there is a hole.
