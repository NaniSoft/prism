## Overview

LogoStrip01 is a single line of short items: the transition band between a hero
and the section that follows it.

All four NaniSoft sites compose this band, immediately after the hero and before
the first numbered section, and each one wrote it as the same thing: a row of
three to six short phrases in muted text, with no heading at all. Four
hand-written strips, four sets of site CSS, and one question each of them
answered differently - whether the strip is a list, a paragraph with separators,
or a decorative row of spans.

It is a `ul`, so a screen reader hears how many items there are before it hears
the first one. That is the whole answer to the question: a strip whose items are
spans in a `div` is announced as one run of text with no count, and a reader
deciding whether to keep listening has nothing to weigh.

## Usage

```tsx
import { LogoStrip01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/logo-strip-01'

<LogoStrip01
  label="Source kinds"
  items={['Broker statements', 'Exchange filings', 'Vendor APIs', 'Archived exports']}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use LogoStrip01 immediately after a hero, to say in one quiet line what the hero
did not have room for.

Use it for three to eight short phrases. Fewer is a sentence, and more is a
paragraph.

### When not to use

Do not use it for logos. This block takes strings and renders them as text, at the
same size as the body. A row of marks is a `ProductGrid01` and a row of images is
neither of these.

Do not use it as a marquee. **The strip does not move.** A band of items that
slides is an entrance animation, and this system has none: motion is state
feedback only. What the four sites wanted from their strips was a second, quieter
statement of what the product is, and repeating the type does that without
anything moving.

Do not use it with more than about eight items. It wraps onto three lines on a
phone and stops being a band; a longer set belongs in a `StackGrid01`.

### Best practices

- Give it a `label`, which is required. It is the accessible name and no visible
  text, so a reader navigating by list knows what they have landed on, and the
  word is yours because a Block that ships no copy ships no reader-facing copy.
- Keep every item to one or two words. The strip is scanned, not read.
- Place it once per page, between the hero and the first section.
- Frame the items in the label, and do not name real organisations in them. A row
  of names is read as a list of who is involved, so a strip carrying four real
  company names hands every reader who sees the page, including a competitor, a
  claim about them. Describe the kinds of thing instead, and let the consumer put
  their own names in.

### Content guidelines

- Write items as names, not as claims. A strip of adjectives is a slogan.
- Keep the register even across the items. One long item among four short ones
  makes the long one the only one read.
- Do not repeat the hero's own words. The strip is the second statement, not the
  first one again.
