## Overview

IndexDetail01 is the master and detail split: an index pane beside a detail pane.
It takes two nodes, places them on two tracks, and holds no selection of its own.
The index pane is where a reader scans and clicks; the detail pane is the answer to
the click. Nothing in the Block connects the two.

The split holds no selection state at all. Selection, routing and which record is
open belong to the consumer, because Prism never imports a router. The index
reports every selection change through its own callback, and reacting to it is the
caller's own read of that callback and a pass of the record it wants into `detail`:

```tsx
const [open, setOpen] = useState<string | null>(null)
const record = records.find((entry) => entry.id === open) ?? null

<IndexDetail01
  index={<DataTable01 rows={rows} onSelectedIdsChange={(ids) => setOpen(ids[0] ?? null)} ... />}
  detail={record ? <RecordDetail01 title={record.name} ... /> : <NothingChosen01 title="Select a record" />}
/>
```

The tracks hold their ratio at every selection state, so clicking a row does not
move the list out from under the cursor. The Block ships no default sentence, no
frame of its own and no loading mark, because it cannot know whether it is at rest.
The detail slot is required: a record that is gone, one this reader may not see and
one whose fetch failed are three causes sharing one address and three different
sentences, so whether the pane is drawn at all is the caller's answer.

The split does not collapse at a narrow viewport. Which pane survives is the
caller's composition rather than a media query deciding a shape, so the narrow
arrangement is one the caller writes: the index alone under `AppShell01`, and the
record as its own screen.

## Usage

```tsx
import { IndexDetail01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/index-detail-01'

<IndexDetail01 index={indexPane} detail={detailPane} />
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it when a reader picks one record from a list and reads it without leaving the
list behind. A mailbox, an invoice register, a project list with its detail beside
it.

### When not to use

Do not use it when the reader should leave the list to read the record. A record
that is its own screen composes `AppShell01`, `PageHeader01` and `RecordDetail01`.

Do not use it to decide which pane is shown at a narrow width. That is a composition
the caller writes; the split renders two panes at every width.

### Best practices

- Hold the selection in the caller. Pass the chosen record into `detail` and let the
  index report the change.
- Put the caller's own `NothingChosen01` in `detail` at rest, or nothing at all. The
  Block ships no fallback.
- Make the index denser when you want it louder. Do not widen its track; a track
  that changes ratio moves the list under the hand that clicked it.
- Compose the index from `DataTable01` and the detail from `RecordDetail01`. Both
  are Blocks the split places and styles nothing inside.

### Content guidelines

- The resting pane says what to do next, in the product's own words: "Select an
  invoice", "Choose a customer".
- A record that cannot be rendered says why, in the caller's own tone and with the
  caller's own route out.
