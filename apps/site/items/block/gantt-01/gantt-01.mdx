## Overview

Gantt01 is a schedule: a left column of task names, a field of bars positioned
against a date range, a scale above, and a rule for today. Every bar is in its
final position at first paint, and the same rows are rendered as a hidden table so
the numbers are readable without sight of the geometry.

The two things that make it correct are worth stating separately, because they are
the two things a hand-rolled version gets wrong.

**The geometry comes from the range rather than from the widest bar.** A field
sized to its own content would put the left edge at the earliest bar that happened
to be there, so a task that starts before every other task would be drawn hanging
off the left of its own chart, clipped by the field, and a reader would see a short
bar where the truth is a long one. So the field is a range: yours, or the earliest
start and the latest end of the tasks. A bar outside a range you passed is clipped
at the edge rather than pushing the field wider, and the table behind the figure
carries its true dates.

**There is no motion, so reduced motion costs the reader nothing.** No draw-in, no
bar that grows from nothing, no scroll effect, no entrance. DESIGN.md's Motion
section refuses entrance animation outright, and a schedule is the case where that
is most obviously right: a bar that arrives is a bar whose arrival a reader has to
wait for before they can read the relationship it has with the bar above it, and
the whole claim of a Gantt is that relationship.

## The hidden table, and its gaps

The bars are `aria-hidden` and the numbers are in a table, which is the rule
`sparkline` and `chart` follow. Everything a schedule draws is a position, and a
position is the one thing a screen reader, a crawler and a print stylesheet cannot
read. The cost is real and worth naming: a table of every task in the document for
a figure that is a few hundred pixels tall.

The table has no column headings, and that is a gap with a reason. This Block has
no words for what a start is called, and inventing "Start" and "End" would be a
claim about your plan. The row head is the task's own name, the caption is your
`label`, and the cells carry your words wherever there are any. The date cells
carry the ISO 8601 reading in the mono face, because this Block was not given a
`locale` and the ones that format dates were.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Gantt01 } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/gantt-01'

const FROM = '2026-09-21T00:00:00Z'
const DAY = 86_400_000

function tickLabel(unit, index) {
  const step = unit === 'day' ? DAY : unit === 'week' ? 7 * DAY : 30 * DAY
  return new Date(new Date(FROM).getTime() + index * step).toLocaleDateString('en-GB', {
    day: 'numeric',
    month: 'short',
  })
}

<Gantt01
  eyebrow="Nexus"
  title="Six weeks of work"
  label="Six weeks of work from 21 September to 2 November 2026"
  tasks={[
    {
      id: 'mapping',
      name: 'Fix the Okta group mapping',
      start: '2026-09-21T00:00:00Z',
      end: '2026-09-25T00:00:00Z',
      progress: 0.8,
      progressLabel: (value) => `${Math.round(value * 100)} percent done`,
      state: 'active',
      stateLabel: 'Being worked',
    },
  ]}
  range={{ from: FROM, to: '2026-11-02T00:00:00Z' }}
  scale="week"
  today="2026-09-29T12:00:00Z"
  todayLabel="Today"
  scaleLabel={tickLabel}
  empty="Nothing is planned."
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it for a plan: a handful of tasks, a range of weeks or months, and a reader
asking "when does this land and what is on top of it".

Use it when the overlap is the point. Two bars on top of each other is a fact a
list cannot show at all, and a schedule whose tasks never overlap is a list.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a queue. A schedule says when, and a queue says what is waiting
now. A backlog that cannot be dated is an `Inbox01`.

Do not use it for more than about forty rows. Past that a schedule is a picture
nobody can scan and the answer is a table, which is `DataTable01`.

Do not use it for a release history. A dated record of what happened is a
`Changelog01`, and it reads bottom to top rather than left to right.

### Best practices

- Pass `range` whenever the reader needs to see empty time, and omit it when the
  work is the whole story. The derived range is the earliest start to the latest
  end, and a caller who wants a quarter that has not started yet has to say so.
- Write `scaleLabel` as a function of `unit`, not as a closure over `scale`. That
  way a scale changed in one place cannot leave a formatter labelling weeks as
  days.
- Keep the labels short. The Block draws one tick per unit with no cap, and the
  labels are your string, so a range that needs shorter labels is a range whose
  formatter returns shorter ones.
- Pass `todayLabel` whenever you pass `today`. An unlabelled rule is a mark a reader
  has to be told the meaning of.
- Pass `stateLabel` with every `state`. The run fails without it, and the fill
  colour is a support for the words rather than a substitute for them.
- Read a bar's exact length from the table when the comparison is between two very
  short tasks. The narrowest a bar is drawn is half a percent of the field, so two
  very short bars are both drawn at about the same width.

### Content guidelines

- `name` is one line. It is a truncated column, not a wrapped one, and a name that
  gets cut is a name a reader cannot match to a ticket.
- `progress` is a fraction between zero and one. Omit it for planned work, and the
  bar is drawn whole in its state colour.
- `progressLabel` is the sentence for that fraction, and a fraction in a cell is a
  figure no reader can act on.
- `todayLabel` is a word a reader would use for the day. "Today" in your language
  is the honest answer.
- `label` is the figure's accessible name and the table's caption, so it should say
  what is plotted and over what period. "Schedule" answers no question.
