## Overview

Signup01 is an account creation: a caller-declared field list, the terms the caller
supplies, a secret with a reveal control and a strength reading that is a caller
function, one submit control, and an announced status region. Every string is passed
in as a prop, and the Block ships no sentence of its own anywhere on the page.

The storefront's version of this pattern is the registration card: a name, an address,
a password with an eye and a strength bar, a line about the terms, a button and a link
back to signing in, whose purpose is to turn a visitor into a shopper with a saved
address. The composition here is that card, unchanged. What it creates is an identity a
machine may act under, and the difference from the storefront shows up in three places:
the reading is yours, the terms are yours, and the sentence on the failure is yours.

## The field list is yours, and that is the design

An account form with a fixed set of fields is a form that decides what a person may say
about themselves, and the decision is invisible in a review because the rendered form
looks complete. It is also the decision that loses the signup: a form that cannot ask
for a workspace name sends a free agent into a product where every entity is scoped to
one, and a form that cannot ask which estate somebody is observing sends half a dozen
accounts onto a queue that belongs to one person.

So `groups` is your declaration in the shared field specification, the same type a
record write form takes: a stable `key`, a required `label` and a required `kind`
drawn from Prism's own controls, with the options a choice carries under that kind and
a `slot` arm for a control this package does not ship. The cost is stated rather than
hidden: one column in the order the list gives, so a consumer that wants a two-column
form composes two of these or reaches for a Page. A choice requires its `options` in
the type and forbids them everywhere else, so a select with nothing in it is caught by
the compiler rather than by a diagnostic.

## The reading is a function, and that is the whole argument

A strength reading is a judgement, and a judgement has two parts that both belong to
your product: the word for it, which is a vocabulary, and the number behind it, which
is a policy. So `password.strength` is a function returning `{ label, value }`, both
yours.

A Block that accepted a label would still have to know the scale to draw a bar, and a
Block that drew the bar would be shipping a rule about what makes a secret acceptable
into every product that installed it, invisibly, in a component library, where no
consumer reads it. The bar itself is a `Progress` and not a `Meter` for the same reason:
a `Meter` needs a tone per threshold, and a tone on a password reading is a claim that
the reader's secret is now a hazard, which is a policy. The cost is that the bar is
drawn against 100 because the Block cannot know your scale, so pass a percentage, and
that a bar which never changes colour cannot warn anybody.

## The terms are optional, and the cost of that is named here

A consumer who can ship this Block without stating terms can make a legal claim they did
not write. The form on the page will read as an account creation with nothing said about
what the account is for, who can see what is entered, and what happens to it afterwards.
So the gate is yours and this Block cannot see it: `terms` is a node you compose, and
the check that it was accepted belongs beside your own submit control, because only you
know whether refusing the terms is a refusal, a disabled control, or a message under
the field.

The alternative was making `terms` required, and the cost of that is real: a product
whose account creation genuinely states no terms, which is a workspace invited through a
link and creates its record on first use, would have had to write a sentence describing
the absence, and that sentence would sit in their source describing something they do
not render. What the Block does supply is the one thing that is not yours to arrange,
which is the place: the terms are drawn between the fields and the control, so a reader
meets them at the moment they are about to press it.

## The three decisions this screen has to make, and why none of them is Prism's

| Decision | What a Block that made it would be |
| --- | --- |
| Whether to score the secret | A security policy shipped into every product that installed it, in a file none of them read. |
| Whether the address is already registered | An account directory, and the enumeration oracle a sign-in surface refuses to be. |
| Whether to send a verification | A transport, a sender, a reputation and a legal claim, none of which is a rendering decision. |

The honest failure is therefore yours as well, and an account creation has more shapes
than one sentence can be true of: the address is already registered, the domain can never
receive mail, a name in the list is not a name, your own field validation refuses an
answer, the terms were not accepted, a breached-secret list caught the phrase, a rate
limit was reached, your identity provider is down, or your database is unreachable. So
`status` is a prop, and there is no `done` flag and no `error` flag in the file.

What the Block does write is one piece of state, and it is yours that triggers it: when
`status.state` reads `done`, the whole form empties, the secret included, because a
secret and a half-written address sitting in a form after the account exists is a worse
thing than a caller who wanted to keep them.

## What the Block refuses

| Refused | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A field with no `label` | Two nameless fields on one page are announced identically. |
| A `select` with no `options` | A question no reader can answer that looks answered only because the control is there and focusable. |
| A blank `password.revealLabel` or `hideLabel` | A button announced as a button, on the one screen a reader is watching while they choose a secret. |
| A `signIn` link with no `label` | The anchor's accessible name would be its destination. |

## Usage

```tsx
'use client'

import { useState } from 'react'
import { Signup01, type Signup01Reading, type Signup01Status } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/blocks/signup-01'

// Your vocabulary and your policy, both in one place.
function read(password: string): Signup01Reading {
  if (password.length < 12) return { label: 'Too short to be worth having', value: 20 }
  if (password.length < 18) return { label: 'Workable, and one more word would help', value: 60 }
  return { label: 'Long, and not a word from a dictionary', value: 95 }
}

export function CreateAccount() {
  const [status, setStatus] = useState<Signup01Status>()

  return (
    <Signup01
      eyebrow="Nexus"
      title="Create an account that may run an estate"
      description="The account acts on your behalf, so it is created with the estate in mind rather than as a shop."
      groups={[
        {
          fields: [
            { key: 'name', kind: 'Input', label: 'Your name', required: true, autoComplete: 'name' },
            { key: 'email', kind: 'Input', label: 'Work email', required: true, autoComplete: 'email' },
            {
              key: 'sector',
              kind: 'NativeSelect',
              label: 'What the estate is',
              required: true,
              options: [
                { value: 'pipeline', label: 'A pipeline' },
                { value: 'market', label: 'A market' },
                { value: 'estate', label: 'An estate' },
              ],
            },
          ],
        },
      ]}
      password={{
        label: 'Secret phrase',
        revealLabel: 'Show the secret phrase',
        hideLabel: 'Hide the secret phrase',
        strength: read,
      }}
      terms={<>You agree to the terms and acknowledge the processing notice.</>}
      submitLabel="Create the account"
      submitting={status?.state === 'working'}
      status={status}
      signIn={{ label: 'Sign in to an account you already have', href: '/login' }}
      onSubmit={async (value) => {
        setStatus({ state: 'working', message: 'Creating the account.' })
        const result = await createAccount(value)
        if (result.registered) {
          setStatus({ state: 'error', message: 'That address already has an account.' })
        } else if (result.undeliverable) {
          setStatus({ state: 'error', message: 'That domain cannot receive mail, so the account would never be confirmed.' })
        } else {
          // The Block empties the form, the secret included, on your own `done`.
          setStatus({ state: 'done', message: 'The account exists.' })
        }
      }}
    />
  )
}
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use it where an account is created by a person filling in a form, and nowhere else. A
workspace invited through a link and created on first use has no form, and a form for it
is a form that asks for information nobody needs.

Declare every field the reader has to give and mark it `required` or not. There is no
third state worth having, and a `required?: boolean` leaves a caller who passed nothing
unable to say which of the two they meant.

Pass a `strength` function unless your product genuinely has no opinion about secret
length. A caller with no function gets no reading, which is the honest arrangement for a
product whose policy document is somebody else's.

Pass `terms` unless your account creation genuinely states no terms, and if you do not,
know that you are the one making the claim.

### When not to use

Do not use it to collect payment details, an address for shipping, or anything else
that belongs to another screen. A signup form that grows into a checkout is two Blocks
that have been confused for one, and the second one needs a different consent.

Do not use it when the reader has already proved who they are and the only thing
missing is a choice. That is `two-factor-01`.

Do not use it for an invitation. An accepted invitation is a control on a row and not a
form, and `invite-user-01` is the shape for it.

Do not expect the Block to check that your fields are the right shape. It forwards
`required` and the autofill hint to the platform and reads nothing back, so the sentence
saying a name is not a name is the browser's or yours.

### Best practices

- Keep the declared field `key`s stable. They are the keys your `onSubmit` receives, and
  renaming one renames it in one place, which is the whole benefit of a declared list.
- Give the platform hints on the fields a password manager keys on: `email`, `name`,
  `tel`, `new-password` for the secret, which this Block already passes.
- Set `status` to `done` only once the account exists. That state is what empties the
  form, and a caller who sets it optimistically will clear a form whose account was
  refused.
- Report the enumeration question on purpose. "That address already has an account" is
  a different disclosure from "check the address and try again", and the choice is
  yours.
- Compose the accepted-terms control yourself, beside your own submit control, and keep
  the `terms` node for the document and the summary.

### Content guidelines

- Write the title as what the account will be able to do. "Create an account that may
  run an estate" names the grant; "Sign up" names the form.
- Write the description as the scope of that authority. A reader is about to give a
  machine the ability to act, and the sentence above the fields is the only place that
  says what the authority covers.
- Write each field label as the thing to type. "Which estate do you observe" beats
  "Estate", and the label is also the field's accessible name.
- Write the strength labels as judgements a person can act on. "Too short to be worth
  having" tells a reader what to do; "weak" is a token.
- Write the terms as the two facts a reader can check, and link the documents from
  there. The Block draws the node where you pass it and nothing else.
- Write the sign-in link as a sentence about where it goes. "Sign in to an account you
  already have" beats "Sign up", because the words a reader can see are the words a
  voice control user has to be able to say out loud.
