## Overview

BlogPostPage is a complete blog post screen: the title, the standfirst, the
byline, the body at the reading measure, and the trail to the neighbouring posts.

All four NaniSoft sites publish a blog, each with four posts and the same
frontmatter: a title, a description, a date and a list of tags. Each one then
rendered that frontmatter in its own component, with its own wrapper, its own
measure and its own treatment of the date, and each of them wrote the trail to the
neighbouring posts separately or not at all.

**The date is two props.** `date` is the words a reader sees and `dateTime` is the
machine value on the `time` element, and they are kept apart because parsing a
formatted date back into an ISO one is a guess about a locale. A wrong date in a
`datetime` attribute is a wrong date in a feed reader and in a search result, and
neither reader can see the string that was wrong.

## Usage

```tsx
import { BlogPostPage } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/pages/blog-post-page'

<BlogPostPage
  title="Every minute, the whole chain"
  description="What live full-chain capture at index scale actually takes."
  date="21 September 2026"
  dateTime="2026-09-21"
  author="NaniSoft"
  tags={[{ label: 'market-data' }, { label: 'kubernetes' }]}
  trailLabels={{ previous: 'Previous', next: 'Next' }}
  trailLabel="More posts"
  previous={{ title: 'One feed to research', href: '/blog/one-feed' }}
  next={{ title: 'Multi-agent research', href: '/blog/multi-agent' }}
  indexLink={{ title: 'All posts', href: '/blog' }}
>
  <p>The body, authored by your own pipeline.</p>
</BlogPostPage>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use BlogPostPage as the body of the route that renders one post, handed the
frontmatter your content pipeline produced.

Use it for every post. A blog whose posts each have their own wrapper is a blog
whose posts drift apart within a year.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a documentation page. Documentation is a set of short pages
about one thing; a post is a dated argument about one thing, and a trail to the
post before it is not a trail through a manual.

Do not use it for a landing page. `MarketingPage` is that composition.

### Best practices

- Pass `dateTime` in `YYYY-MM-DD`. It is the value a feed reader and a search
  engine order by, and it is the one that is wrong silently.
- The body is `children`, held by `Prose`. Your pipeline owns the document; this
  owns the measure and the rhythm. Do not parse it into an array of paragraphs to
  hand over - that is a second representation of something your pipeline already
  has.
- Render only the halves of the trail you have. A previous link on the oldest post
  is a link to the first post a reader has already read, and a next link on the
  newest is a promise the site cannot keep.
- `trailLabel` and `trailLabels` are required alongside a neighbour. They are words
  a reader reads and hears, and a Page that ships no copy ships no reader-facing
  copy either: a site that files posts newest-last says the opposite pair.
- Use `footer` for what comes after the trail: related posts, a newsletter form, a
  closing call to action. It is a slot because every one of those is a Block, and a
  Page that took a Block per prop would be a Page whose interface lists the whole
  catalogue.
- Pass `indexLink` with the words and the route your archive lives at. The page
  ships neither.

### Content guidelines

- Write the standfirst as one or two sentences a reader can stop at. It is what an
  index, a feed and a search result show.
- Keep tags to nouns. A tag is a category a reader browses, not a sentence.
- Keep the title to one line at the largest viewport unless the post is the only
  thing on the page.
