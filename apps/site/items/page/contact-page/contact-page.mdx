## Overview

ContactPage is a complete contact screen: the page heading, a form whose fields the
caller declares, a band of offices and regions, and a closing band for the questions
a reader should not have to ask to find this screen.

| Order | Region | Who owns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the page heading, at `h1` | the Page |
| 2 | the form band, one step below | the Page, from the caller's `fields` |
| 3 | the offices, as facts | the Page, from the caller's `offices` |
| 4 | the questions | the caller, passed in as `footer` |

**The offices are the part that makes this a page rather than a `contact-01`.** A
contact block is a band: one form, in one column, with the caller's fields. A
contact screen is the band, the places, and the questions, and the two extras are
what a reader actually arrives with, because a reader who wants to write has a
fallback and a reader who has run out of patience has a map.

**The offices are facts and not controls.** A postal line is a string a reader
copies, `detail` is a node because the honest thing under a place is sometimes a
sentence and sometimes a table of hours, and the only link a card may carry is your
own map link, with words on it. A screen that turned its regions into navigation
would be telling a reader that a place is a topic they can read about, and the only
topic any of them has is how to get there.

**The status is `Contact01`'s own, and this page ships no success sentence.** A
contact request can be delivered, held by a filter, filed by a bot, refused as a
duplicate and answered, and one sentence cannot be true of all five. So you own the
promise and write the words.

## Usage

```tsx
import { ContactPage } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/pages/contact-page'

<ContactPage
  title="Tell us what is going wrong"
  description="Who reads this and what happens next."
  form={{ title: 'Write to the estate desk' }}
  fields={[
    { id: 'name', label: 'Your name', type: 'text', required: true },
    { id: 'email', label: 'Where to reply', type: 'email', required: true },
    {
      id: 'topic',
      label: 'What this is about',
      type: 'select',
      required: true,
      options: [{ value: 'collector', label: 'A collector that will not start' }],
    },
    { id: 'message', label: 'What happened', type: 'textarea', required: true },
  ]}
  onSubmit={send}
  submitLabel="Send it"
  consent="We keep what you write until the request is closed."
  status={status}
  officesLabel="Where we are"
  offices={[
    {
      id: 'london',
      name: 'London',
      lines: ['Nexus Software Ltd', '2 Bevis Marks', 'London EC3A 7BD'],
      mapHref: '/offices/london',
      mapLabel: 'Open the map',
    },
  ]}
  footer={<Faq01 {...questions} />}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use ContactPage for a public route whose job is to get an enquiry to a person: a
support desk, a sales enquiry, a security disclosure.

Use it with the offices omitted. A product with one desk and one form passes no
`offices` and the screen is the heading, the form and the questions.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a sign-in problem. `ForgotPassword01`, `Login01` and
`MagicLink01` are blocks that own their own transport, and a contact form is a slow
way to reset a secret.

Do not use it for a newsletter. `Newsletter01` is the block for that, and it needs
a consent sentence this screen does not require.

Do not use it when there is exactly one postal address beside exactly one form.
`Contact01`'s `address` arm puts the two side by side in one band, which is the
better arrangement for one address, and composing the block is four lines.

### Best practices

- Every field carries a `label`, and every field is yours. The field list is the
  design: a form with a fixed set of fields decides what a reader may say, and the
  decision is invisible in a screenshot because the rendered form looks complete.
- A `select` field carries `options`, and the page throws without them. A control
  with nothing in it is a question no reader can answer that looks answered only
  because it is there and focusable.
- `officesLabel` is required wherever `offices` is. "Offices", "Where we are" and
  "Locations" are three products' words, and a row of addresses with nothing above
  it is the region every contact screen forgets to name.
- `mapLabel` is required wherever `mapHref` is, and forbidden where it is not. A map
  control announced by its coordinates is punctuation rather than a name.
- Pass `headingLevel="h2"` when the screen is embedded under a heading the consumer
  already owns.

### Content guidelines

- The page heading says what the reader should write about. "Tell us what is going
  wrong" beats "Contact us", which is a word about the page rather than about the
  reader's situation.
- The form band heading names the desk, not the action. "Write to the estate desk"
  says who will read it.
- The `description` says who reads the message and what happens next. It is the
  sentence that decides whether somebody writes at all.
- An office's `detail` is the thing a reader needs and cannot infer: the hours the
  desk is read, the languages it answers in, the nearest station.
