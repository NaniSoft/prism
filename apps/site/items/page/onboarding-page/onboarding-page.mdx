## Overview

OnboardingPage is a complete setup screen. It composes the checklist with a way to
ask for help and a closing band, in one of two arrangements, and passes the
checklist its own steps, its progress figure and its empty sentence.

| Region | Who owns it |
| --- | --- |
| the page heading, drawn by the checklist at `h1` | the caller's `title` |
| the checklist and its progress figure | the Page, from the caller's `steps` |
| the way out of a setup that is going wrong | the caller, passed in as `help` |
| the closing band | the caller, passed in as `footer` |

**The distinction from `onboarding-01` is the whole argument for this page.** The
Block is the checklist. This is the screen. A checklist is a list with a figure
above it, and a list is a thing a reader arrives at. A screen is a heading, a list,
and a way out of the situation the list describes, and a reader who is stuck does not
go looking for the checklist: they arrive at the screen, because the screen is the
only address they were ever given. So the page composes the checklist with a way to
ask, and `help` is why it is a page and not a block: a checklist with no escape is a
dead end a reader has to close the tab to leave, and closing the tab is the worst
outcome a setup screen can have, because the reader does not come back.

**The `h1` is the checklist's own section heading.** A title the page drew above the
checklist would be the same words twice on one screen, and it would put a second
section of vertical padding between the heading and the one list the heading is
about.

## Usage

```tsx
import { OnboardingPage } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/pages/onboarding-page'

<OnboardingPage
  title="Three things, then your estate is running"
  description="One or two sentences about what the reader will have when the list is finished."
  steps={[
    { id: 'workspace', name: 'Create the workspace', state: 'done', stateLabel: 'Done yesterday' },
    { id: 'collector', name: 'Connect the first collector', state: 'current', stateLabel: 'You are here' },
    {
      id: 'approval',
      name: 'Approve the permission set',
      state: 'blocked',
      stateLabel: 'Waiting on an administrator, since Tuesday',
    },
  ]}
  progressLabel={(done, total) => `${done} of ${total} done`}
  completion="Nothing else is needed. The first run starts itself."
  empty="There is nothing to set up yet, so this estate is already running."
  help={<Help01 {...helpIndex} />}
  footer={<Newsletter01 {...newsletter} />}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use OnboardingPage for a first-run screen or a return visit to a setup that is not
finished. It works when the reader's question is "what is left" or "what is
blocking me", which are the two questions a setup screen exists to answer.

Use `layout="split"` when the reader has been here before and needs the answer
rather than the route. Use the default `centred` for a first run, where there is
one thing to do and nothing competing for the reader.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a task with one step. A list of one is a form, and `provisioning-01`
or a `FieldMap01` is the block for it.

Do not use it for a wizard that owns the reader's answers as it goes. This screen
takes a list of states and draws it; a wizard holds the state and moves the reader
through it, which is a different kind of surface.

Do not compose it without a `help` region for a setup a reader can get stuck in.
The block alone is legal and is the right answer for a setup of two steps where
neither can block, but the whole argument for the page is the way out.

### Best practices

- Every step carries a `stateLabel`. `Onboarding01` refuses a step without one, and
  the reason is that a mark with no sentence beside it is a shape a reader can only
  see. "Connected your warehouse" and "Waiting on your warehouse" are the same
  position and different things to do about it.
- Use `blocked` the moment a step is waiting on somebody else. It is the one state
  that is a fact about this run rather than a position in the order, and a reader
  who believes the product is broken stops trusting the product.
- `progressLabel` is a function, not a string, because the phrase has a plural and
  every language words it differently. Never compose it in the package.
- `empty` is required. A setup with no steps is either a product with nothing to set
  up or a query that returned nothing, and those are opposite claims.
- Pass `headingLevel="h2"` when the screen is embedded under a heading the consumer
  already owns.

### Content guidelines

- A step name is a noun phrase a reader would use in conversation. "Connect the
  first collector", not "Collector connection configuration".
- A `description` says what this step involves, not what the product is. It is
  drawn under the name and read by a reader who has arrived at the step.
- A `stateLabel` is the product's own sentence about the state. It is the fact; the
  mark beside it is a faster read of the same thing.
- `completion` says what happens when the last step is done, in one sentence.
