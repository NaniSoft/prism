## Overview

StatusPage is a complete service-status screen: the estate's own state, the services under it with
their own states, the recent incidents with their updates, and slots for your ways to follow along.

**The overall state is your judgement and never a computation over the rows.** An estate can be
degrading in a way no single service admits to: every node is individually healthy, every job
finished, and the product is unusable because what a reader came for is the sum of those services
rather than any of them. A Page that computed the overall state from its own rows would be forced
into one of two failures, and both are on the same screen. It would over-report, painting a red
banner over one degraded node out of forty that nobody can feel, which trains a reader to ignore
the banner. Or it would under-report, drawing green beside forty green rows while the product is
down, which is the failure that costs a trust once. Neither is something a design system can decide
for a product, because the weight of a service and the shape of a dependency between them are facts
about the product and none of them are visible from a row.

**Two vocabularies are on this screen and only one of them is closed.** The estate is one of five
states, because an estate has one. The four incident states are closed, because an incident is a
lifecycle and a lifecycle has one right order: looked into, cause found, fix watched, over. A
*service's* state is a plain string, because that is a product's own vocabulary with as many words
in it as the product likes. The consequence is visible and is the right one: the estate and each
incident get a dot, because Prism owns how those two look, and a service's state is drawn as your
words with the machine value on the row as `data-state`.

**Prism formats no date here.** `at` is your value and `dateLabel` is your reading of it, because
"12 minutes ago" is a sentence in the reader's language and not a number with a suffix. The cost is
that a browser cannot read the incident date off this Page as a machine value; compose
`RelativeTime` where your own `datetime` is when you need that.

## Usage

```tsx
import { StatusPage } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/pages/status-page'

<StatusPage
  title="Nexus Estate"
  description="Capture, inference and the ledger that records both."
  overall={{
    state: 'degraded',
    label: 'Degraded performance',
    note: 'Inference requests are queued and the queue is about eleven minutes deep.',
  }}
  services={[
    { id: 'capture', name: 'Live capture', state: 'running', stateLabel: 'Running, behind' },
    { id: 'inference', name: 'Inference', state: 'degraded', stateLabel: 'Degraded' },
  ]}
  incidentsLabel="Recent incidents"
  incidents={[]}
  empty="No incident has been opened on this estate in the last ninety days."
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use StatusPage as the body of the route your product's monitoring publishes. It is a whole screen
and a whole screen is what a status page is: an estate's own state, what that state is made of,
and what has happened recently.

Use it for the public page. For a status page inside a product's own chrome, compose the same Blocks
under your own heading instead, so the product's navigation and this screen's data are not fighting
over one layout.

### When not to use

Do not use it for a component's own health inside a dashboard. That is `StatusLedger01` beside a
chart, and the difference is that a dashboard row is evidence while a status page is a judgement
about the whole.

Do not use it as an uptime monitor you poll. This Page renders the props it is given and holds no
timer, so a screen that refreshes itself is a screen whose banner moves under a reader for reasons
they cannot see.

### Best practices

- Take the overall state from the same place your alerting takes it, and make it the same
  judgement. The one thing this Page will not do is reconcile your banner with your own rows.
- Order the services yourself. The order is a claim about what matters when everything is degraded,
  and a Page that sorted by severity would be making it with a rule you cannot see.
- Pass `href` and `hrefLabel` together on a service or an incident, or neither. A link with no words
  is a control a reader has to guess at.
- Keep `incidents` newest first and trim it. The band is a reader's short history, not an archive;
  link to the rest from the `updates` slot.
- Put your archive, your postmortems and your subscribe form in the slots rather than composing them
  into the Page. A status page that offers a subscribe control it did not build is a control that
  cannot act.
- It is a server Component. It fetches nothing, holds no state and imports no router, and the one
  function it takes is called while it renders, so a server route can pass it.

### Content guidelines

- Write the overall label as the reader would say it. "Degraded performance" and "All systems
  operational" are both sentences in your product's voice.
- Write `empty` as an all-clear, not as an absence. This is the one line on a status page a reader
  is guaranteed to read, and a status page that cannot say "nothing has happened" is one nobody
  keeps open in a second tab.
- Keep the note to one sentence: what is affected, when it will be over, or what a reader should do.
  Pick one.
- Keep each incident update to what changed. It is a timeline, and a reader checking whether a fix
  is holding is reading the last entry far more often than the first.
