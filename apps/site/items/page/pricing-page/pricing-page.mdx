## Overview

PricingPage is a complete pricing screen. It owns the page heading, an optional
notice that the prices moved, the row of plans, and the comparison band, and it
leaves everything under the comparison to the caller in whatever order they want.

| Order | Region | Who owns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the page heading, at `h1` | the Page |
| 2 | the change note, when there is one | the Page |
| 3 | the row of plans | the Page, from the caller's `plans` |
| 4 | the comparison band, its heading and its link | the Page |
| 5 | the matrix | the caller, passed in as `matrix` |
| 6 | everything below | the caller, passed in as `bands` |

**The order is the decision the page makes, and the order is a claim about how a
reader decides.** A reader who has not chosen wants the shape of the choice before
the detail of it. A reader who has been choosing for a week wants the detail, and
can already see the shape. So the change note sits between the heading and the
prices rather than below them, and the matrix sits below the row rather than beside
it.

**The order costs something.** A product whose objections belong above the plans
cannot reorder this screen. The honest answer is to compose two pages and put the
seam where the objection was, which is two page rhythms where the author wanted
one. That is the correct price of an opinionated order; an `order` prop would have
moved the decision to every call site, which is to say it would have stopped being
a page.

**The plan row is drawn by the page rather than delegated to `pricing-01`.** That
Block takes a formatted `price: string` and an optional `badge`, and this page
needs `Intl` to do the formatting and needs a highlighted plan to carry a required
word. A page that composed it anyway would have had to re-declare both.

## Usage

```tsx
import { PricingPage } from '@nanisoft/prism-ui/pages/pricing-page'

<PricingPage
  title="What it costs"
  description="One sentence saying what every plan has in common."
  changeNote={{
    title: 'The metered run price moved on 1 September.',
    href: '/changelog/2026-09-01',
    hrefLabel: 'Read the full note',
  }}
  plans={[
    {
      id: 'starter',
      name: 'Starter',
      price: { amount: 0, currency: 'GBP', period: 'a month' },
      action: { label: 'Create a workspace', href: '/signup' },
    },
    {
      id: 'team',
      name: 'Team',
      featured: true,
      featuredLabel: 'What most teams choose',
      price: { amount: 480, currency: 'GBP', compareAt: 600, period: 'a month' },
      action: { label: 'Start on Team', href: '/signup?plan=team' },
    },
  ]}
  comparison={{ title: 'Every feature, plan by plan' }}
  compareLink={{ label: 'See the full specification', href: '/plans/specification' }}
  matrix={<PricingCompare01 plans={matrixPlans} groups={matrixGroups} labels={labels} />}
/>
```

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use PricingPage for a public pricing route whose job is to let a reader compare
what a product sells and choose one of the plans. It works when the row is the
argument: the reader arrives knowing what the thing is and wants to know what it
costs.

Use it with the comparison band omitted. A product with three plans and no
exhaustive matrix passes no `matrix` and no `comparison`, and the page ends on the
row.

### When not to use

Do not use it inside an authenticated screen. A plan row on a billing page competes
with the task that page exists to support, and a reader who is already paying does
not need to be walked towards a plan.

Do not use it to show usage rates. `RateCard01` is the block for a unit price, a
tier and an overage, and a pricing screen for a metered product is often both.

Do not use it when the plans are not comparable. A page that forces two unrelated
things into one row has made a claim about how they relate, which is a fact about
the product and not about the layout.

### Best practices

- `featured` marks at most one plan. Two highlighted plans throw, and so does a
  pricing screen with no plans at all, because both are claims the page refuses to
  make for you.
- `featuredLabel` is required wherever `featured` is set and forbidden where it is
  not. A plan marked as the one to choose with no word for it is a highlight with
  no meaning, and the words are yours: "Most chosen", "Best value" and
  "Recommended" are three products' sentences.
- Give the matrix no `title` of its own. The page owns the band heading, and a
  matrix that draws a second one announces the same section twice.
- Put the `Faq01` and the closing `Offer01` in `bands`, in that order. The page
  holds no band of its own below the comparison, so the questions and the offer are
  content rather than structure.
- Compose at one level deeper when the page is embedded under a heading the
  consumer already owns. Pass `headingLevel="h2"` and the whole outline follows.

### Content guidelines

- The page heading says what the reader is deciding, not what the page is. "What it
  costs" beats "Pricing".
- A plan's `summary` is one sentence about who the plan is for. A plan name plus a
  feature list is not a summary.
- A `note` under a price is a qualification, not a second summary. Billing terms,
  minimum commitments and the exchange rate a reader should check.
- The change note says what moved and when. It is not a marketing band, and it
  carries the link to the full note rather than the whole explanation.
