An empty state is the first screen of most products, and the one most often designed
last. It is also the only screen every reader is guaranteed to see, because nobody
installs a product with data already in it.

**An empty state is not an error and must not read like one.** Red ink, a warning
icon and a sentence beginning "Error" tell a reader they have done something wrong
when they have simply not started. The tone is the same as a page that has content:
this is the normal state of a new account, and it should read as ordinary.

## Name what is absent, then offer the way onward

`EmptyState01` takes the name of the thing that is missing and an action. Both are
props, because a Block ships no words: a state that says "No data" is telling every
consumer's reader the same thing, which is the thing this system exists to end.

The action is the half that is usually missing. An empty state with no action is a
notice, and a reader who has just been told their account is empty is left to work
out what to do about it, which they cannot do if they do not yet know the product's
vocabulary.

One action, and it is the one that resolves the absence. Two actions on an empty
state means the reader has to choose before they have any context to choose with.

## Say which filter emptied it, and offer to clear it

An empty result set from a query and an empty table are different sentences, and
conflating them is the most common empty-state bug.

- **Filtered**: names the filter and offers to remove it. "No invoices for March."
  with a `Button` that clears the month. The reader arrived with an intention and
  the product is what lost it.
- **Unfiltered**: states the fact about the product. "No invoices yet." The reader
  arrived to look and there is genuinely nothing.

The second with a "Clear filters" action is a page that blames the reader for the
product's state, and the first with no action is a dead end.

## Do not fill the space to make it look designed

A grid of empty card outlines, a placeholder illustration, or a chart with no data is
a page pretending to have content. It is worse than the honest empty state, because
the reader spends a moment working out whether the thing they are looking at is real.

If the space genuinely needs to not be empty, put something **useful** there rather
than something shaped like the thing that is missing. A `Card` holding one real
example, or a `Prose` paragraph explaining what will appear, earns its place; a grey
rectangle does not.

## One empty state per surface, and only on the surface that is empty

A table that is empty and a page that is empty are different states, and showing both
at once means the reader sees two explanations of one fact. Put the state on the
narrowest surface that is actually empty: an empty table inside a populated page is a
table state, not a page state.
