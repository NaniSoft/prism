---
title: An application shell with a settings area
description: "Three screens, and the one that is a Page rather than a Block."
arranges:
  - MarketingPage
  - DashboardPage
  - SettingsPage
---

The interesting part of this arrangement is that one of the three is not really a
peer of the other two. A dashboard and a marketing page are each a whole screen. A
settings area is a screen that a product grows into, and the arrangement is usually
the first thing that gives it away.

## `SettingsPage` is the one that constrains the others

`SettingsPage` owns a rail, and the rail is what makes a settings area a settings
area rather than a form. The consequence runs the other way: once a product has a
rail, every screen beside it has to say where it sits in that rail, or the reader has
two places to look for the same setting.

So an application that has `SettingsPage` has committed to a navigation shape, and the
honest time to notice is while choosing it. The alternative, a settings panel inside
the dashboard's own layout, is a smaller commitment and a worse product past about
twenty settings.

## The dashboard is a frame, and the frame is not Prism's

`DashboardPage` composes Blocks and receives its data. It does not fetch, it does not
route and it does not decide what a metric is. A dashboard whose data arrives as
props is one whose refresh policy belongs to the consumer, and that is deliberate: a
polling interval is a product decision about cost and freshness, and a design system
that hardcoded one would have made the wrong call for every product but one.

## Marketing is a separate screen, not a tab

Keeping `MarketingPage` out of the application is the point of having it as its own
Page. A marketing page reached through the same shell as the dashboard is a page
whose reader has already logged in, which is not who that page is for, and it inherits
a navigation offering destinations that person cannot use.

So the arrangement is: a public screen with no rail, and an application with a rail.
Two shells is not a duplication when the two audiences have nothing in common.

## What the consumer still owns

Which routes these three are, what a reader is allowed to reach before signing in,
what a metric means, and the words on every screen. The arrangement says which screen
goes where and why; it says nothing about what any of them contain.
