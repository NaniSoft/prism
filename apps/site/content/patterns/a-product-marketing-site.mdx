A Template is not a copy of a site. It is the **arrangement**: which screens exist,
what order they are reached in, and what a reader can get to from where. The
arrangement is the published `DocsNavEntry` union rather than a new vocabulary,
which is the shape `DocsShell` already takes and the shape a consumer site already
adapts its own tree into.

That reuse is the point. A site that grew its own navigation vocabulary would have a
second thing to keep in step with the first, and the disagreement would be invisible
until a reader met it.

## Two screens, and the second one is a decision

`MarketingPage` is the public surface: hero, features, pricing, a closing call to
action. It fetches nothing and owns no route, so a consumer mounts it wherever the
frameworks' routing puts a page.

`DocsShell` is the second screen, and whether to have one is a decision rather than a
default. A product whose API is part of the product needs it. A product whose
"docs" are three paragraphs does not, and shipping a second shell it does not need
gives a reader a place to look that has nothing in it.

## The order is the argument

A marketing site's navigation is a claim about what the product is. Putting pricing
beside features says the product is something you choose between plans; burying it
says the price is a detail. The order is not a layout decision and it does not belong
to the design system, which is why a Template states it and does not derive it.

## What the consumer owns

Everything a site needs that the arrangement cannot know: the routes, the data, the
authentication, the pack and the mode, and the real words. The Template says which
screen goes where and why. A consumer that has to override the arrangement to fit its
product has been given the wrong one, and that is worth knowing before the first
deploy rather than after.
