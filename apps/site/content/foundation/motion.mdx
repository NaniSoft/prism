---
title: Motion
description: "Two laws: feedback on a short scale, and a running figure on a cycle scale, both by token."
---

Motion has two laws, and they are different laws rather than one law with an
exception. The distinction is falsifiable: stop the animation and ask whether
the figure is still true and still legible.

**Feedback is a response.** Something happened because the reader did it. They
are waiting for an answer, so the answer is fast, decelerating and never
overshoots. The scale is `fast` 80ms, `base` 160ms and `slow` 280ms, with
`ease-out` and `ease-in-out`. A component names a duration token and an easing
token rather than a value, and spatial movement is guarded with `motion-safe:`.

**A cycle is a demonstration.** A marker crosses a rail, a reticle crosses a
series, a wavefront crosses a field. The reader did not cause it and is not
waiting for it: the figure is making a claim about a mechanism and the motion is
how the claim is made visible. If stopping the animation leaves nothing true and
nothing legible, the motion was the figure, and the right component is one that
says it is atmosphere.

The cycle scale is separate because the two measure different things by a factor
of twenty-five, and one name cannot mean both. `ambient` is in seconds, and the
emitted contract measures it with a floor of one second: a cycle faster than that
is unreadable before it is understood.

Three rules bound it, and none of them is a matter of taste.

- **Nothing is hidden.** No ambient rule sets `opacity: 0`, and nothing waits
  for a script, an intersection or a timer. Every figure is complete at first
  paint, so a reader with scripting off, a print stylesheet or a crawler sees the
  whole drawing.
- **Reduced motion removes the movement, not the figure.** The
  `prefers-reduced-motion` block is one `animation: none`, and because every
  resting state is the full form, that reader gets the same figure, still.
- **Compositor properties only.** Every keyframe animates `transform` or
  `opacity`. Nothing in the layer animates a layout or paint property.

A keyframe is a mechanism and lives in the component package's stylesheet; a
duration is a value and lives in the token source. They meet at exactly one
place, an `animation` shorthand whose length is `var(--ambient-travel)`, so
neither can invent the other. The six `prism-ambient-*` classes exist so that a
component picks a cycle by naming a mechanism and never writes a timing.
