---
title: Motion
description: "Three laws: feedback on a short scale, a running figure on a cycle scale, and an arrival bound to the reader's own scroll, all by token."
---

Motion has three laws, and they are different laws rather than one law with
exceptions. The distinction is falsifiable: stop the animation and ask whether
the figure is still true and still legible.

**Feedback is a response.** Something happened because the reader did it. They
are waiting for an answer, so the answer is fast, decelerating and never
overshoots. The scale is `fast` 80ms, `base` 160ms and `slow` 280ms, with
`ease-out` and `ease-in-out`. A component names a duration token and an easing
token rather than a value, and it does not decide reduced motion for itself.

**A cycle is a demonstration.** A marker crosses a rail, a reticle crosses a
series, a wavefront crosses a field, light crosses a ground. The reader did not
cause it and is not waiting for it: the figure is making a claim about a
mechanism and the motion is how the claim is made visible. If stopping the
animation leaves nothing true and nothing legible, the motion was the figure,
and the right component is one that says it is atmosphere.

**An arrival is bound to the reader's own scroll.** A section a reader scrolls
to may meet them, and the meeting is the third law rather than a loosened
second: it runs on CSS scroll-driven animations, so no script decides when it
plays and no timer can play it early; the content exists in the document from
the first paint; a browser that does not ship the timeline declines the whole
rule and shows the section simply there; and the universal reduced-motion rule
hands that reader the same still page. The one script-driven figure in the
system, `ParticleField`, follows the same test from the other side: it renders
its complete still figure on the server and the script only adds the movement.

The cycle scale is separate because it measures a different thing by a factor
of twenty-five, and one name cannot mean both. `ambient` is in seconds, and the
emitted contract measures it with a floor of one second: a cycle faster than
that is unreadable before it is understood.

Three rules bound the ambient layer, and none of them is a matter of taste.

- **Nothing in the ambient layer is hidden.** No ambient rule sets `opacity: 0`
  and no ambient figure waits for a script or a timer. Every figure is complete
  at first paint, so a reader with scripting off, a print stylesheet or a
  crawler sees the whole drawing. The reveal layer is the one exception ever
  granted, and it is granted to the reader's own scroll position rather than to
  a script: it lives behind a capability check, and collapses to the fully
  visible form the moment anything, including reduced motion, declines to run
  it.
- **Reduced motion removes the movement, not the figure.** The
  `prefers-reduced-motion` block is one unlayered rule setting `animation: none`
  and `transition: none` on the universal selector, and because every resting
  state is the full form, that reader gets the same figure, still.
- **Compositor properties only.** Every ambient keyframe animates `transform`
  or `opacity`. Nothing in the layer animates a layout or paint property. The
  one named exception is the spectrum ink on display type, which animates a
  gradient position because text cannot be moved behind its own gradient, and
  the exception is a class in the stylesheet with a scope, not a licence.

One rule covers the whole package rather than each component guarding itself, and
that is a correction rather than a preference. A `motion-safe:` variant adds a
rule inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` and never removes one, so a
guarded transition beside an unguarded one ran at every setting: three components
were guarding, all three were different shapes, and one was guarding nothing. A
duration cannot express the policy either, because it cannot tell a colour that
changed from something that moved. What a reader with the setting on keeps is
everything that is not movement, which is to say every state and every
announcement, without the time.

A keyframe is a mechanism and lives in the component package's stylesheet; a
duration is a value and lives in the token source. They meet at exactly one
place, an `animation` shorthand whose length is `var(--ambient-travel)`, so
neither can invent the other. The eight `prism-ambient-*` classes exist so that
a component picks a cycle by naming a mechanism and never writes a timing.
