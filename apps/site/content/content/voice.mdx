---
title: Voice
description: "NaniSoft's fixed voice qualities, and how tone shifts by context."
---

Voice is fixed. It does not change from feature to feature or from screen to
screen. Every piece of product copy MUST be clear, human and helpful. All three,
always.

Tone is the part that moves. The same voice sounds different in an error than in
a success message, and the context chooses the tone.

## Clear

Write like a knowledgeable colleague explaining something, not a manual.

- RULE: Use plain words.
- RULE: Be as short as the meaning allows.
- RULE: Say exactly what to do or where to go.

## Human

Write to a person, in the first and second person.

- RULE: Use "you" for the reader and "we" for NaniSoft.
- RULE: Use contractions where they read naturally.
- RULE: Avoid jargon unless the glossary defines it, and define it on first use.

## Helpful

Every message has a job. If it has no job, delete it.

- RULE: Tell the reader what happened and what to do next.
- RULE: Never blame the reader.
- RULE: Never leave a dead end. Every error MUST name a recovery.

## Choosing the tone

| Context | Reader's state | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Errors, failures, empty states | Frustrated or confused | Matter-of-fact and direct | "No projects found. Change or clear your filters." |
| Completed setup, saved work | Pleased | Plainly positive, no celebration | "Your changes are saved." |
| Destructive actions | Cautious | Explicit and calm | "Delete this project? This cannot be undone." |
| First run, onboarding | Curious | Brief and encouraging | "Add your first project to see it here." |

RULE: A success message MUST state what changed, not congratulate the reader.
RULE: A destructive confirmation MUST name the thing and the consequence.

## Judgment

These qualities are principles. Apply them with judgment, and use
[Writing mechanics](/content/writing-mechanics) for the concrete rules on
capitalisation, punctuation and length.
