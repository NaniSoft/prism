---
title: Audience
description: "The three people NaniSoft product copy serves, and what each one needs from a sentence."
---

Product copy serves three audiences. A piece of text MUST land for the person who
reads it in that moment, and the three do not always want the same thing.

## The operator

The operator uses the product to finish a job, often repeatedly. They know the
interface and resent being told what they already know, but they need
confirmation that a consequential action will do what they expect.

- They read errors, confirmations and destructive-action warnings closely.
- They skip onboarding and explanation.
- RULE: For the operator, be brief and specific. State the consequence, not the
  mechanism.

## The evaluator

The evaluator is deciding whether the product does what they need. They meet the
product once, with a task in mind, and they are quick to leave.

- They read empty states, first-run text and headings.
- They need the next step to be obvious.
- RULE: For the evaluator, name the outcome and give exactly one clear next
  action.

## The builder

The builder integrates NaniSoft into something they own. They read documentation
more than product copy, but the two meet in import errors, configuration screens
and generated code.

- They need exact names: packs, tokens, props and package paths.
- They are unforgiving of a label that does not match the API.
- RULE: For the builder, use the exact, cased term from the glossary. Do not
  paraphrase a name of anything.

## Reading level

RULE: Write for a general professional audience. Prefer common words, active
voice and a single idea per sentence.
