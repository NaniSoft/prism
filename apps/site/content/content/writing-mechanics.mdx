---
title: Writing mechanics
description: "Capitalisation, punctuation, grammar and structure rules for NaniSoft product copy."
---

These are the mechanical rules. They are narrower than voice and tone, and they
are the ones a reviewer can check without judgment.

## Capitalisation

RULE: Write sentences and UI labels in sentence case. Capitalise the first word
and proper nouns only.

RULE: Use title case only for the product's own name and for a pack name when it
is a proper noun in a heading.

RULE: Do not capitalise a generic word to make it look important. "Dashboard",
"settings" and "project" are lowercase in a sentence.

## Punctuation

RULE: Use the serial comma in a list of three or more items.

RULE: Use periods in complete sentences and in helper text that is a sentence.
Omit the period in a label, a button, a heading and a short fragment.

RULE: Never use an exclamation mark twice in one message, and never use one in an
error.

RULE: Use a colon to introduce a list, and a semicolon when two independent
clauses are close enough to share a sentence.

RULE: Do not use em dashes or en dashes. Use a period, a comma, a colon or a
plain hyphen.

## Grammar

RULE: Write in the active voice.

RULE: Address the reader as "you". Refer to NaniSoft as "we".

RULE: Use the present tense for what the product does, and the past tense only
for something that already happened.

RULE: Keep one idea per sentence, and keep a sentence under 25 words where the
meaning allows.

## Syntax and structure

RULE: Lead each screen or dialog with the outcome, then the action.

RULE: Put the most important information first, and the next step last.

RULE: Use a numbered list when order matters, and a bulleted list when it does
not.

RULE: Prefer plain language. Replace a jargon word with a common one unless the
jargon word is a defined product term in the
[Product vocabulary](/content/product-vocabulary).
